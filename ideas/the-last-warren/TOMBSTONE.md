## The verdict

Tokyo Jungle's living organ is not the animals, the city or the combat. It is this:

> **You can't stay, because staying runs out. You can't leave cheaply, because leaving is
> where you die. And where you finally settle decides what the next generation is.**

The red team found the organ inside tower defense — *accumulated strength creates
exposure*. Tokyo Jungle is a corpse that already had that organ, already turned the player
into the thing being hunted, and already made **settling a decision about who you become**.
It just buried it under a novelty premise (a Pomeranian in post-human Shibuya) that the
world remembered instead.

## How it played (the parts that matter here)

*From the research pass; to be checked against a full teardown before anything is quoted
publicly.*

- **Survival mode is the game.** You play an animal and its descendants across in-game
  years, in a Tokyo emptied of people.
- **Hunger drains constantly.** You can't hold still; food forces you across the map.
- **Territory is claimed, not owned.** You mark spots in an area to claim it, which is what
  lets you breed there.
- **Breeding is the save point.** With territory claimed, you find a mate and go to a nest.
  The mate's quality decides the litter, and the litter is the next generation: your pack,
  and in practice your lives.
- **Age is a second clock.** Your animal gets older; breed before it runs out or the line ends.
- **Predators hunt, prey hides.** Stealth in cover, exposure in the open.

## Why it died

Platform, not design. A PS3 exclusive, digital-only in the West, from a small studio
attached to a first-party division that no longer exists. Nobody owns the job of bringing
it back, so nobody does. The premise was the hook and also the ceiling: it's remembered as
"the Pomeranian game", not as the stay-or-go machine underneath.

## Cremation — what we are refusing to reproduce

Burned, and named so it can't creep back in at 2am:

- **Animals, and post-apocalyptic Tokyo.** The setting is the corpse's face. It stays dead.
- **Real-time action combat and the stealth-in-grass layer.** We are tick-based and visible.
- **Hunger as a survival meter.** Our pressure comes from the hunters, not from a bar.
- **Species unlocks, costumes, collectibles and DLC.** None of it.
- **Mate-quality RNG** as a slot machine for stats.
- **A score at the end.** Tokyo Jungle ranked your survival in years; we pay off in what happened.

## Unstuck — the organ somewhere completely different

Strip the animal, keep the decision, and turn it into a board you can read at a glance:
dots on a grid, a troop counter going up, hunters stepping one block a tick. The question
Tokyo Jungle asked with a pack of dogs, asked again with nothing but time:

> **How much stronger do I need to be before I move?**

## What nobody used properly

Tokyo Jungle tied **where you settle** to **what you become**, then spent that idea on
stat bonuses nobody looked at, behind a premise everybody looked at. The Last Warren puts
it at the centre: a Warren is not a location, it is *a version of yourself*, and moving is
choosing a different one under pressure.

## Open questions

The red team's five still stand (see the provisional file): is complete information fun ·
how vulnerable is transit · what "found" means · whether Warrens change *how* you play ·
whether attack earns its place. Added by the tombstone:

6. **Is there a second clock?** Tokyo Jungle had two: hunger and age. We have one, the
   hunters. Is one enough, or does the troop need something that runs out too?
7. **Does the troop carry anything between Warrens?** In Tokyo Jungle the next generation
   inherited. Does a troop that leaves a Combat Warren stay combat-shaped, fade, or reset?
8. **What does the ending say?** If the run ends when the last warren falls, the payoff is
   the sentence about how it fell, not the number of ticks survived.
