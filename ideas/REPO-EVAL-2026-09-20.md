# Three repos, read for what they carry

**`netmobster/sc-engine`** · **`netmobster/hardpass`** · **`netmobster/smudgegame`**

⛔ **Read, not run.** Nothing here was executed; this is what the documents say, and what
of it is liftable. Where a claim is about our code it names the file.

> ### The headline: `sc-engine` describes a discipline the SEREN web port is currently breaking, and `hardpass` contains the persona format SEREN has been missing all day.

---

# 1 · `sc-engine` — the one that matters most

**What it is.** A spec for a three-frame social-media universe: one invention goes in,
three posts fall out, one per frame. *"A concept can't answer that. A concept is an
assertion. What answers it is a system with slots."*

## 1.1 ⛔ It names a mistake in `seren/web/weave.py`

From `AGENT-ARCHITECTURE.md`, on separating the writer from the scorer:

> **Regenerate is a fresh call, not a retry.** A writer told "that failed" in the same
> context will defend the thing it wrote. **Clear the context and re-run.**
>
> **Revision passes dimension names only.** *"VOICE, SHAREABILITY"* — not the scorer's
> reasoning. **Reasoning is an argument, and the writer will write toward it rather than
> toward the work.**

**Our weaver does the opposite of both.** `weave.mend()` takes the auditor's `problems`
list — the full reasons — and hands them back to the same model to patch its own campaign.
⚠️ **That is a retry with the argument attached**, which is precisely the shape this
document says produces a defence rather than a rewrite.

⭐ **Cheap to test and cheap to fix:** on a refusal, throw the campaign away and re-weave
from the same hand with the failed *dimension names* only. **We would find out inside one
evening whether mended campaigns are worse than re-dealt ones.**

## 1.2 ⛔ And a second one: who is allowed to read the rubric

> **Why the writer never reads `scoring.md`:** a writer that knows the rubric writes to the
> rubric. It will manufacture a visible tension and a legible second thing because those
> are the scored dimensions — and produce a post that **scores well and reads like a form.**
> The rubric measures the work; it does not brief it.

**This is the single most useful sentence in the three repos**, and it is a warning about
something we were about to do. Today's `ARCHITECTURE-NON-CODE.md` proposes an auditor that
refuses a persona with fewer than three moves — fine, that is a build-time artefact check.
⚠️ **But the moment any of `audit.py`'s criteria reach the weaver's prompt, the weaver
starts writing campaigns that pass the audit** rather than campaigns worth playing. The
audit is currently downstream and must stay there.

## 1.3 ⭐ CLOCK — the fix for our dead fronts, stated better than I stated it

> Every post carries a counter that **drifts one direction and is never discussed on
> camera.** […] **The arc lives in state, not plot.** State is legible from a single post
> — you don't need to have watched it move to read where it currently is. Which matters,
> because posts arrive out of order. **A plot-arc dies on a shuffled feed. A state-arc
> doesn't care.**

We found this morning that **nothing in `seren/web/` ticks a front clock** — the fronts are
decoration. This says the *other* half: a clock is only worth having if its position is
**readable from a single turn without exposition.** ⭐ And the shuffled-feed argument is
exactly a player who misses three sessions, or plays out of order — which is every real
campaign.

## 1.4 MAP — relationship state carried in form, never exposition

One line, and it is the whole of what our companions are missing. Not *"Lira trusts you
more now"* — the trust shows in how she answers.

## 1.5 The registers table

Each frame carries numbers: **World 90 / Performance 20** for the Adjuster, **100 / 40**
for the Hazard Officer. ⭐ **Dials with values, per voice** — which is `persona-format.md`'s
seven dials, arrived at independently for a different medium. Worth comparing the two lists
before we rebuild SEREN's persona generator; two independent derivations agreeing on a dial
is evidence, and disagreeing is a question.

# 2 · `hardpass` — the persona format, already written

**What it is.** Six reality-based investors who stress-test a startup idea. Nine axes,
eight scored, one conditional. Pre-build; design and architecture only.

## 2.1 ⭐ The character sheets are what SEREN's DM-persona.md should have been

`characters/01-marcus-webb.md`, in full shape: **Archetype · Role · Tone · Background ·
Primary Axes · Kill Shots · Firing Threshold · Signature Line.**

Compare what our weaver emits for a DM: a name, `**Told like:**`, `**Which means:**`.

⭐ **And "Firing Threshold" is the piece nobody in SEREN has:**

> **Fires on** market logic, timing, and behaviour change assumptions. **Goes quiet when**
> the founder demonstrates genuine market insight — not just TAM math, but *why this
> market, why now.* **That silence is respect.**

**A move that says when NOT to act, and what the not-acting means.** Today's delta report
called out that SEREN resolves every turn completely and has no pacing permission anywhere.
**This is the missing rule, in Jay's own writing, in another repo.**

## 2.2 Kill Shots are a Voice section that actually works

Five verbatim lines rather than adjectives. *"Is this a market that exists or a market
you're hoping to create? Because those are very different bets."* ⭐ **You can hear him.**
The 42 role templates in SEREN's corpus have a Voice paragraph each; **Kill Shots suggest
they should also carry two or three sayable lines.**

## 2.3 The conditional axis

Eight axes always scored; **Two-Sided Risk auto-fires on detection.** A rule that only
exists when the situation contains it. ⚠️ Worth stealing for fronts: most conditions should
be checked every beat, and some should only exist once something has happened.

## 2.4 Output revealed post-session only

The scorecard is not shown during play. ⭐ **Same instinct as Ferret Bowling's "show the
working" eye — post-hoc only, so it cannot become an optimiser.** Three separate projects
have now independently arrived at *the measurement must not be visible while you play.*

# 3 · `smudgegame` — shipped craft rather than architecture

**What it is.** A tower-defence game built in Lovable — TypeScript, React, canvas. Waves,
towers, gold, upgrade lines, worlds with palette overrides. Far more *built* than the other
two, and far less *specified*: the README is Lovable boilerplate.

## 3.1 ⭐ Named synergies between pairs

`james_lola` (gun + ink), `marshall_notbetsy` (sniper + chain). Two towers together unlock a
named effect, shown as a pulsing gold ring and an amber badge. **The name is doing the
work** — a player remembers "James and Lola" and never remembers "+40% damage vs slowed."

⚠️ **Note Not-Betsy**, which is also in IDEAS.md's Pip entry from *Dear Satan*. **A cast is
already migrating between projects**, which is either the start of a shared world or a
naming collision to be decided on purpose.

## 3.2 Event-driven dialogue tables

`WAVE_COMMENTARY` · `SYNERGY_MESSAGES` · `LIFE_LOST_MESSAGES` · `KILL_MILESTONE_MESSAGES` ·
`WAVE_CLEARED_MESSAGES`. **Lines keyed to events rather than a narrator deciding when to
speak.** ⭐ That is the cheapest possible version of the "something reacts" problem, and it
needs no model at all.

## 3.3 A world that repaints the whole UI

Alberta replaces the dark chrome with warm sand, turquoise and sunset orange — *"no cold
blues, no matte black anywhere"* — HUD included, for the rest of the run. **A world change
that is felt rather than announced.**

## 3.4 The Virgin Margarita

A one-time purchase that makes **clicking anywhere on the canvas** earn gold. A tower
defence briefly becoming a clicker. ⭐ Worth noting as a pattern: *a purchase that changes
what kind of game you are playing for a while*, rather than adding a number.

# 6 · ⭐ `FRICTION-LOG.md` — which I called the most valuable file, then went and read

**It is.** *"What got killed, and why. Kept rather than deleted — the mess is the truth, and
what was rejected says as much as what survived."* Two findings, and **both are the shape of
today.**

## F-1 — fixing one dimension broke another

> VOICE went 1→3 and SECOND THING went 3→1 **in the same edit**: rewriting the exclusion as
> one clause deleted the line carrying the contradiction.
> **Re-score the whole matrix after a revision, never only the named dimension.**

⛔ **That is the pattern of the entire 2026-09-20 session, three times over:**

| the fix | what it broke |
|---|---|
| Hidden facts into `dm_side`, so the gate could see secrets | the gate began holding the DM for quoting **the player's own words** |
| A `settled` guard so a hand could not pay out twice | its early return skipped releasing the turn lock and **deadlocked the table** |
| The player's See button made legal when owing nothing | **the identical bug left in Noke's code**, so he burned the raise cap every hand |

**Every one was a correct fix that broke a neighbour, and every one was found by a human
playing rather than by me checking.** F-1 is the rule that catches all three: **after a fix,
re-check the whole thing, not the thing you fixed.**

## F-2 — a strong total masked a core failure

> **23/27 reads *ship*** while carrying a **1 on the most load-bearing dimension.**
> Totals need a **per-dimension floor.**

⚠️ **`seren/web/audit.py` counts problems and refuses at two.** So a campaign carrying one
catastrophic fault passes. A floor on the dimensions that matter is small, and it is absent.

## And the part that is hardest to do

> ### Deliberately not fixed
> **The gates in `canon/scoring.md` still have the F-2 flaw. That is on purpose.**
> A repo that ships a *fixed* scorer implies one was built and run at volume. **It wasn't.**

⭐ **The flaw is left in, labelled, with the reason** — rather than quietly patched so the
repo looks finished. Same discipline as `FIRST-PLAY.md` keeping its four faults, and the
reason both are worth reading a month later.

## ALPHA-01, in one line

> **Step 2 was Adjuster-shaped.** […] The step had to be bent to work, which means it was
> **written from one frame and generalised without checking.**

⚠️ **The generalisation bug** — a rule authored against one case and assumed to cover the
rest. *(See also: a See button fixed for one player and not the other.)*

## ⛔ And the sentence that settles the isolation argument

> **Neither was reachable by self-scoring.** The V1 score was written knowing the intent and
> **did not surface either.**

Not a principle — **a measurement.** The same model scoring its own work missed two real
defects a blind pass caught at once. ⭐ **That is the empirical case for §1.1 and §1.2, and
the reason to treat them as findings rather than taste.**

# 7 · Salvage — Jay: *"are either of those games salvageable?"*

**Four candidates, in order of how much finished work is sitting there.**

## ⭐ PRISM — the most salvageable thing in the estate, and it is not in IDEAS.md

`PROJECTS/UNTITLED BROWSER GAME/` — a **gem-merge tower defence**, shelved 2026-08-24.
*"Playable prototype, balance-tested, unshipped."* One file, vanilla JS, **no build step,
no dependencies** — open `index.html` and it runs.

**The mechanic, and it is the good part:** a gem is an RGB vector plus a level. **Hue is
which stats it gives** (R damage, G fire rate, B range), **saturation is purity**, size is
level. Merging two same-level gems averages their colour.

> **A perfectly balanced gem renders as a muddy grey rock and sits in your drawer judging
> you.** You can read your whole inventory at a glance with zero icons — a drawer of vivid
> jewels means you played disciplined, a drawer of grey pebbles means you panic-merged all
> game.

⭐ **The colour IS the stat sheet.** No tooltips, no numbers, and the drawer is a record of
how you behaved under pressure — **the Unstuck payoff shape**: you leave with what
happened, not a score.

### Why it is salvageable rather than merely unfinished

**Four structural problems were found by bot-testing, fixed, and written down** under a
heading that says *do not re-derive this*:

1. ⛔ **Linking could never make purity win** — at equal power a pure trio scored 0.87x a
   balanced one, and *"no tuning fixes this."* **Fixed with RESONANCE**: a link pays in
   proportion to how *different* two gems are. Pure red beside pure green blazes; two grey
   rocks resonate not at all.
2. **Build spots, not mana, are the scarcity that forces merging.** A bot with 29 spots
   reached wave 21 having merged **zero** times. Cut to 16.
3. **Nothing created pressure to make a bad merge**, so purity cost nothing and bought
   nothing. **Fixed: gems mostly ARRIVE**, in colours you did not choose.
4. **Drops had to stay behind the merge ladder** or every strategy played identically.

**Where it landed:** two bots, opposite merge rules, three seeds each — **within ~5%,
neither dominates.** And a noted accident: `P_GROWTH` (2.2) ≈ max resonance (2.21), so one
extra level is worth almost exactly one full rainbow cluster. **The decision balances on a
knife edge and the doc says so.**

### And it has already done the Unstuck teardown

The backlog names GemCraft's own failure: *"dual gems are made for raw damage with the
effect incidental, triple gems are a strict trap. **Their purity axis collapsed.** Ours
avoids that by having no universal damage stat — colour IS the stat. Don't reintroduce a
universal stat."*

⭐ **The method, already applied**: tombstone named, the exact thing that collapsed
identified, and the fix built into the foundation rather than bolted on.

**What it needs:** the map editor (the engine is already data-driven off `WAYPOINTS` and
`BUILD_SPOTS`, so it is mostly UI), then levels. **Gem bombs are the dignified exit for
grey rocks** — your mistakes become emergency ordnance, and the doc calls it cheap and
probably great feel.

## 2 · `hardpass` — designed, and IDEAS.md is out of date about it

IDEAS.md says **"On deck; not designed."** ⚠️ **It is designed.** Six full character
sheets, nine evaluation axes with a scoring model, homepage copy, feature notes, a product
brief, and `hardpass-v0.1.jsx`. **What is missing is a build, not a design.**

**And the salvage is a decision rather than work:** the repo is a *tool* that kills bad
startup ideas. IDEAS.md wants a *game* where every investor passes and you find out why.
**Same six characters, same nine axes, opposite posture** — the tool tells you the truth,
the game makes you earn it. The characters port either way.

## 3 · `smudgegame` — finished enough that the question changes

`GameEngine.ts` 20KB, `renderer.ts` 54KB, nine systems, eight UI components, prestige shop,
achievements, save, worlds, quirks, super towers. **A complete tower defence**, not a
prototype.

⛔ **So the question is not whether it can be salvaged but whether it is an Unstuck game.**
It carries no tombstone and no refusal — it is a good TD with a cast. That is a fine thing
to ship and it is not the studio's stated argument.

⭐ **And it collides with PRISM.** Two tower defences: one finished and generic, one
unfinished with an idea. **Do not build both.**

## 4 · `TD-ROGUELITE` — concept only, worth keeping as a lens

Same folder as PRISM, 2026-08-24, **no code**: *"a tower defence where you are management,
not the operator. You prepare, you commit, you press go, and then you watch. Your skill is
expressed before the wave, not during it."*

⚠️ **Nothing to salvage — but note the verb.** You do not control the thing, which is
Orbis and Elsewhere as well. **Three games now share it**, and that is either the studio's
signature or a rut. Worth naming on purpose rather than discovering later.
