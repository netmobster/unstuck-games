INFINITE
FRONTIER

A 2D top-down roguelike tower defense where the player builds the battlefield one tile at a time, drafts temporary power, and survives as long as possible.

Single-player first2D top-downMath-generated visualsInfinite run mapRun resets on deathNo runtime AI
Core thesis

The board is the build.

Traditional tower defense asks the player to optimize a map someone else designed. Infinite Frontier asks the player to design the route, shape the battlefield, draft the tools, and defend the thing they created.

The map grows throughout a run. Death resets it. The player is continuously solving a spatial problem with an imperfect, randomized toolkit.

01 / The pacing model

Combat is fast. Decisions should be quick, contextual, and constrained by the board—not long inventory-management pauses.

Every wave
~30 sec

Draft & defend

A wave resolves quickly. Then draw three reward cards and take one: typically two new towers and one upgrade, but the exact mix is randomized. Choose, place or apply, and get back into play.

Every milestone
10 waves

Boss → relic → tile

Wave 10, 20, 30, and onward is a boss. Beat it to draft/receive a relic and choose one of three new tiles. Place and rotate it to expand the route and battlefield. The map changes in meaningful chunks, about every five minutes of combat.

When you die
Run ends

Reset & return

The map, towers, resources, cards gained, upgrades, potions, and relics reset. Eligible unlocks and records persist. Start again on a tiny map with a wider pool of possible strategies.

02 / The fast loop

01 · WAVE ENDSBrief result; core damage, kills, reward trigger.
02 · DRAFTSee 3 cards; choose 1 in seconds.
03 · APPLYPlace a tower in a limited number of viable spaces, apply an upgrade, or hold/use a potion.
04 · NEXT WAVECombat resumes quickly. The chosen reward changes the next encounter.
05 · MILESTONEAfter wave 10 boss: relic + tile choice + rotate/place, then continue.

The intended feel is closer to a fast Slay the Spire reward decision than a lengthy build menu. Board constraints make the choices concrete: a powerful tower is only valuable if there is somewhere useful to put it.

03 / Three layers of decisions

Wave · tactical

Reward card

Choose one of three offers from the currently unlocked pool. Common offer types: new tower, upgrade bonus, consumable potion. Example: two towers and an upgrade. The draft should be fast, legible, and contextual enough to avoid three useless options.

Milestone · strategic

Relic + tile

Beat the boss to gain a relic that meaningfully changes the current run and a choice of three tiles, each with route geometry, a benefit, and a consequence. Place and rotate the chosen tile to extend the connected path.

Run · meta

Unlock possibilities

Death resets the attempt. Across runs, unlock new towers, upgrades, potions, relics, tile families, and enemy types through milestones/achievements. Unlock breadth and alternate strategies—not permanent raw stat inflation. Rapid death should not be the optimal unlock strategy.

04 / The battlefield

Tiny opening

Begin on one randomly selected 9×9 world tile/chunk, with fog of war. Only two or three good tower positions may exist. Space scarcity makes every reward contextual and gives the first few waves immediate tension. The world map is assembled from these chunks; it is not one 9×9 board.

Tile grammar

Draw three candidate tiles randomly. Each combines path geometry and a built-in bonus/consequence (terrain, adjacency, cost, range, risk, or future placement). Select one, place it at the frontier, rotate it to connect. Placement is committed.

One continuous run map

Expansion happens at boss milestones, not every wave. The same board grows continuously; there are no acts or map transitions. The full map becomes a strategic overview and a record of this run's design.

Path-length hypothesis: Long routes increase firing time, but should not automatically dominate. First test geometry, coverage, tower budgets, terrain, and enemy traits. Do not add arbitrary long-path penalties before observing play.

05 / Reward economy

Wave cards are temporaryNew towers, upgrades, and potions belong to the current run. No permanent accumulation of tower power across lives.
Relics are run-definingA relic is awarded after each boss milestone. It bends a rule or creates a synergy for the current run, then disappears on death.
Pool unlocks are lateralUnlocks add possibilities rather than direct numerical superiority. Unlock in curated batches so the reward pool doesn't become too diluted.
Keep agency in the drawRNG chooses offers and threats; the player chooses a response. Maintain a few broadly useful options, some build synergies, and occasional build-defining offers.

06 / Visual and technical direction

Locked

2D top-down

Flat grid viewed directly from above. No isometric camera, 3D scene, or perspective presentation in the prototype. Board geometry and enemy paths must be immediately readable.

Locked

Math-generated visuals

Render the world from simple geometry, rules, and parameters: grid cells, paths, towers, projectiles, enemies, effects, fog, and UI. Prefer generated/vector-like shapes and procedural effects over hand-painted assets or an asset-heavy pipeline.

Locked

Simulation-first

Deterministic rules resolve path connectivity, enemy movement, tower targeting, damage, resources, and run state. No runtime AI is needed for core gameplay. Seeded randomness can make runs reproducible.

The art direction should come from clean silhouettes, color, animation, impact feedback, and composition—not texture detail. The strategic overview should be readable at a glance, even as the map grows.

07 / Prototype sequence

PROTOTYPE 0

Tile grammar

Flat grid; one randomized 9×9 starting chunk with core and spawn; four path shapes; three 9×9 expansion-chunk offers; rotation; legal boundary connectivity; fog reveal.

PROTOTYPE 1

One wave

Three tower types, a few enemy profiles, build phase, 30-second wave, readable projectile/damage feedback.

PROTOTYPE 2

Draft loop

Three reward cards after every wave; quick application; tower-space constraints; wave difficulty tests different layouts.

PROTOTYPE 3

Wave 10 boss, relic + tile milestone, death/reset, results, and a small lateral unlock pool. Observe multiple runs before expanding content.

The thesis to test

Does building the board make every run feel different?

Every tile changes the battlefield. Every wave draft changes the tools. Every milestone adds a relic and another piece of route. The player's skill is choosing well under spatial, economic, and RNG constraints.

For Claude Code: Start with the smallest playable gray-box that tests grid geometry, tile selection/rotation, route connectivity, and tower placement. Keep systems modular and deterministic. Do not build the full content library, meta-progression, elaborate menus, or final visual polish before the core loop is playable.

09 / Implementation appendix for Claude Code

Concrete starting points, not permission to overbuild. Inspect the existing repository and its stack first; follow established conventions where they exist. Keep the simulation deterministic and the first prototype small.

Data model

Separate state from rendering

The grid, tile ports, route graph, tower placements, enemy positions, health, resources, wave number, reward offers, relics, and run state are authoritative game data. Rendering reads this state; drawing code must not decide gameplay outcomes.

Tile grammar

Four-bit connection masks

Represent cardinal ports with N = 1, E = 2, S = 4, W = 8. A straight N–S tile is 5; an N–E bend is 3; a three-way junction N–E–S is 7; a cross is 15. Rotate the mask by moving each port clockwise and wrapping W back to N.

Validation

Check the route graph

Adjacent ports must be reciprocal: an east port on one cell requires a west port on its neighbor. After placement, validate the full route from spawn to core and reject illegal placements before committing. Local adjacency alone does not prove the whole route is valid.

Clockwise rotation rule: with N=bit 0, E=bit 1, S=bit 2, W=bit 3, rotate a four-bit mask using ((mask << 1) & 15) | (mask >> 3). Apply it once per 90° clockwise turn. Test all 16 masks and four rotations; rotating four times must return the original mask.

Core state machine

A · COMBAT

Wave running

Enemies traverse the validated route; towers acquire targets and deal damage. Resolve the wave result and check whether the core has been destroyed.

B · REGULAR

Reward draft

If the wave is not a milestone, offer three reward cards and let the player choose one. Apply it quickly, then begin the next wave.

C · MILESTONE

Boss → relic → tile

On waves 10, 20, 30, etc., run the boss encounter. If defeated, award a relic and offer three tiles; select, rotate, and place one legally before the next wave.

D · RUN END

Death / reset

If the core is destroyed, end the run, record results, grant eligible unlocks, clear run-only state, and start a fresh tiny map.

Implementation detail to settle explicitly: whether the boss wave also grants the ordinary three-card reward. The concept currently guarantees the milestone relic + tile; do not silently add or omit a second reward. Make it a simple configurable rule.

Build order: prove the geometry before combat

Prototype 0 — grid and route

Render the small top-down grid and fog; select a candidate tile; rotate and place it; display ports clearly; validate reciprocal connections and spawn-to-core reachability. Include automated tests for rotation and route validation.

Prototype 1 — one deterministic wave

Add a small set of enemy profiles, route-following movement, three tower archetypes, range/targeting, damage, and a readable wave result. Use a fixed seed or fixed test scenario while debugging.

Prototype 2 — fast reward draft

Offer three cards after regular waves, select one, apply its effect, and resume quickly. Make tower placement legal and obvious; avoid a large inventory or lengthy modal flow.

Prototype 3 — milestone and reset

Trigger a boss every tenth wave; on victory grant the relic and tile choice; on death reset run-only state and retain only eligible unlocks/records.

Visual implementation notes

Readable geometry firstUse simple vector/canvas-style shapes for grid, paths, towers, enemies, projectiles, range indicators, fog, and health. Silhouette and contrast matter more than decorative detail.
Use visuals as feedbackShow route direction, tower range, target selection, hits, slow effects, leaks, core damage, and valid/invalid tile placement clearly. Animation should communicate simulation state.
2D top-down onlyNo isometric or 3D presentation in the prototype. Keep the strategic overview and tactical board consistent so players can read the map at either scale.
Choose the renderer pragmaticallyInspect the repository and runtime before choosing Canvas, SVG, or another existing rendering path. Do not introduce WebGL or a new framework without a demonstrated need.
Guardrail for implementation: Do not build all content, meta-progression, sophisticated enemy AI, multiplayer, or final polish yet. First deliver a playable loop that proves legal tile placement, a readable route, meaningful tower placement, and one wave that can be won or lost.

10 / World chunks and seeded generation

Important spatial rule: every world tile is a 9×9 chunk of grid cells. The world is a larger grid of these chunks. Do not implement each expansion as a single cell on the combat grid.

Starting chunk

Randomized Tile 1

A new run begins on one randomly selected/generated 9×9 starting chunk. It must guarantee a valid spawn-to-core route and enough legal build space for the opening encounter. Fog hides unrevealed cells/frontier as appropriate.

Expansion chunk

One of three offers

At each boss milestone, offer three candidate 9×9 chunks. Each has internal route geometry, legal boundary connection points, terrain/placement characteristics, and a bonus/consequence. The player chooses one, rotates it, and attaches it to the current world frontier.

World coordinates

Chunk grid + local grid

Track each chunk at world/chunk coordinates and each cell at local coordinates 0–8 in both axes. Convert between them through one well-tested helper; keep chunk placement and cell placement distinct in code.

Seed strategy: many possible chunks, not 10,000 hand-authored maps

Use deterministic generation from a seed, a small set of validated chunk archetypes, and controlled variations. A target catalogue of 10,000 distinct seeded chunk instances is possible, but it is not a Prototype 0 requirement and should not mean manually authoring 10,000 maps.

Seeded offer generationDerive each offer from the run seed plus a stable offer counter (or equivalent deterministic stream). The same seed and state should reproduce the same three offers.
Validated archetypesStart with a small set of known-valid 9×9 layouts: straight-through, bend, offset, and other controlled route patterns. Generate variations in terrain, bonuses, consequences, and orientation within constraints.
Validate before offeringEvery generated chunk must have compatible boundary ports, a traversable internal route, no accidental route breaks, and sensible buildable space. Regenerate or fall back to a known-valid chunk if validation fails.
ReproducibilityStore the run seed and the chosen chunk seed/definition. Do not depend on a single global random stream whose results change when unrelated visual effects consume randomness.
Prototype constraint: begin with a handful of hand-validated 9×9 chunk archetypes and seeded variations. Prove chunk attachment, route continuity across chunk boundaries, rotation, tower placement, and combat first. Scale the generation catalogue only after those rules work.
