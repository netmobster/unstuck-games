Ferret Bowling — in-run UI handoff

This file is the spec. Three screens (DURING RUN, ZOOMED, AFTER RUN) in one flat HTML file, no build, no dependencies except Google Fonts. Everything in the house is CSS — the only images are three Lucy poses.

The one rule

The player is here to watch. Every pixel is either Lucy, the house she is changing, or the smallest possible readout. The moment the screen starts explaining the simulation instead of showing it, the game is a spreadsheet and we have lost. When in doubt, cut the readout and make the house bigger.

Corollary: no monospace anywhere. The prototype used mono labels because it was a dev build, and mono reads as telemetry. It was the single biggest cause of "flat and boring". Handwriting reads as someone who lives here.

Material system — a risograph dollhouse

The house is printed paper, not a UI. Five rules produce the whole look; they are cheap and they compose.

  1. Overprint. Every coloured shape is mix-blend-mode: multiply over the paper. Where two inks cross, they darken. Do not soften anything with opacity — let the ink stack.
  2. Misregistration. A duplicate layer offset 2–3px in a second ink at 20–40%. Use it on the masthead and the cage blanket only. Everywhere would read as a broken screen.
  3. Hand-cut edges. Asymmetric radii — border-radius: 9px 5px 11px 6px. Never a uniform radius; uniform radius is the tell that a computer drew it. Generate the four values per element from a seeded RNG so a given room keeps its shape across runs.
  4. Printed shadow. box-shadow: 5px 6px 0 rgba(15,92,87,.2) — hard, offset, tinted with a second ink. Zero blur. Blurred shadows are the other tell.
  5. Grain. One overlay per paper sheet: radial-gradient(rgba(43,36,29,.45) .5px, transparent .6px) at background-size:3px 3px, opacity:.2, multiply, pointer-events:none, above everything.

Type roles — three faces, no overlap

FaceUsed forNever
Alfa Slab OneStamps: room names, counters, buttons, the mastheadSentences

Handwritten labels are absolutely positioned with white-space: nowrap. They may overlap the artwork a little — that is correct, they were scribbled on top. They must never overlap each other.

Must be driven by the sim, not by art

Each of these is a real value the simulation already has or can cheaply produce. If any becomes a static decoration, the house stops feeling alive and you may as well delete it.

Thing on screenDriven by
Route line, drawing itselfPolyline of her actual positions, revealed with stroke-dashoffset as the run advances. Post-run it stays and turns gold.
Dashed ghost outlinesEvery object carries origin and current. If they differ, draw the dashed outline at origin. Persists across runs — this is the main proof that the house remembers.
Hoard under the couchOne shape per treasure (cap the drawn pile at ~6, keep counting). +1 pops on acquisition only.
Cage blanketMood as a single value — one ink and one stripe pattern per mood, the way grime works in Bad Monkeys. Label underneath reads "cage — smug today".
Slam alertOnly outrageous-flagged events, never ordinary journal lines. Hard budget: max one per ~20s or it stops meaning anything.
"new!" / "+12" markersA firstSeenRun field on objects and journal entries.
Ambient motionFree, but keep it per-room and low-amplitude: dust in the window light, fridge lamp flicker, plant sway, the pendant lamp. If it ever competes with Lucy for the eye, slow it down.

Where I departed from the brief, and why

  • Four counters survive (runs / known / treasures / habits). The brief bans RPG statistics and it is right, but these are collection counts: they say "there is more Lucy to find", not "Lucy is 74% better". No percentages, no affinities, no XP, ever. If someone adds a fifth counter, ask what it makes the player do.
  • One bar: energy. It is really the run clock wearing a costume. Keep it because "she is running out of steam" is a narrative fact. If playtesters start optimising against it, delete it.
  • Locked rooms show a threshold ("opens at 24 known"). That is the brief's own progression rule made visible. It must stay a journal-entry count — never a currency, never a purchase.
  • Prep is capped at two. The brief lists five preps. A cap makes prep a choice; no cap makes it a loadout, and a loadout is optimisation. Tune the number, keep the cap.
  • No pause. Speeds are 1× / 4× / 16× / nap. Pausing an autonomous creature implies you are driving her.
  • Zoom replaced a second view. An earlier pass had a separate cross-section "peephole" panel under the plan. Cut: a second art style for the same room doubles art cost and splits the player's model of the house. Zoom is now literally the same drawing, scaled, with the chrome removed. One house, two distances.
  • The journal feed shows four entries, not a scroll. A scrollbar invites reading instead of watching. Full history belongs on the post-run screen.

Ideas I did not build

  • Desire paths. Her route should thin and darken where she has walked before, so the house slowly shows her habits without a word of copy. Cheapest possible "you know Lucy now" feedback.
  • The paper ages. After ~10 runs the sheet itself picks up more grain, a coffee ring, a torn corner. One value, same shape as the Bad Monkeys grime system.
  • A different ink for the unprecedented. When the sim produces a behaviour with no prior instance, print that journal card in a colour nothing else uses. That is the "wait, she hasn't done that before" moment made visible, and it is one if.
  • Lift the couch. Click it, the hoard lays itself out on the rug. Discovery, not inventory management.
  • Per-room ambience. Audio is by far the cheapest way to make the house feel occupied — the fridge hums, the hallway is dead quiet, the bedroom has a clock.
  • Nap-skip should still narrate. Skipping ahead shouldn't silently jump; print the two or three things that happened while you weren't looking.

Assets

  • Three poses used: walk (plan view, moving), curious (zoomed), lazy (post-run, asleep in the cage). The source PNGs have a text label baked into the bottom ~20% — cropped here with a fixed-height overflow:hidden wrapper. Re-export clean when you can.
  • Everything else is CSS. No image assets are needed for the house, the furniture or the hoard, and that is deliberate — a new object costs a div, not a drawing.
  • Fonts: Alfa Slab One, Caveat, Archivo (Google Fonts).
  • Palette: paper #f2ead3, ink #2b241d, teal #0f5c57, tomato #d8352a, marigold #ffd23f, sage #7a8a5e, terracotta #c67139, wood #8a5a2b. Eight inks, no more — a riso press has a limited drum count and the constraint is doing real work here.

Suggested updates — the Ferret Bowling homepage

The live page sells a launcher: aim, distance in metres, rolls, top speed, surfaces, boosters with percentage buffs. The game is now a house, a memory and a journal. Most of the page describes a different product — and two sections actively contradict the red-team rule.

Fix first — these contradict the design brief

  1. "WHAT SHE PICKS UP" is a stat block. +40% DISTANCE, +1 ROLL PER SECOND, RICOCHET · 3 WALLS is exactly the "feed → stat increase → better run" loop the brief forbids, and it is the most quotable copy on the page. Replace with WHAT SHE TAKES: the same objects, described as things she has opinions about and keeps. "Your slipper. She has had it for four runs and shows no sign of returning it."
  2. The run-log panel (DISTANCE / ROLLS DOWN / TOP SPEED / AIRBORNE) measures a throw. Replace with what a run actually produces: RUN 14 · 74s · 3 new things learned · 1 new treasure · 1 habit confirmed.
  3. "Whatever she knocks over in the hallway, you spend on a longer hallway." That is a currency loop. The house opens on understanding, not spend. Suggested: "Whatever she does in the hallway, you write down. Eventually the bathroom door opens."

  • THE JOURNAL. Three real entries as paper, in the game's voice, with nothing explaining them. "Lucy stuffed the dish towel into her favourite shoe. It is a nest now. It was always going to be a nest." This is the most convincing thing the game produces and the site does not show it.
  • HER TREASURES — UNDER THE COUCH. A short list that reads as a hoard, with one line making the persistence explicit: "She has had your slipper since run 6."

Do not touch

  • Lucy's bio. It is the reason anyone cares, and "TOP SPEED 19.4 km/h" stays — that is a fact about a real ferret, not a game stat.
  • The $5 section and "no ferret behind a paywall".
  • The Galápagos tombstone.
  • The voice throughout. It is already right; only the nouns need changing.

Visual

If the game goes riso, the site should take the same ink set and the same handwriting — overprinted multiply fills, hard offset shadows, Caveat annotations in the margins. Right now the site is flat cream and Alfa Slab, which is the same problem the game UI had. One shared eight-ink palette across both would do most of the work.
