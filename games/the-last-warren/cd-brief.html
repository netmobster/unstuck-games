2 · The whole game, red-team grade

Code: games/the-last-warren/ on ideas/the-last-warren. Tombstone: Tokyo Jungle (2012). Its organ is that where you settle decides what you become. The values below are the tuned rules (src/tuned.js).

Time and space.

  • A 28×18 grid. A night is 54 ticks, with at most 8 nights.
  • Entering a tile costs movement points: open 2, road 1, forest 3, lake 6 for the troop and impassable for hunters, mountain impassable.
  • Points banked per tick: troop in transit 3, hunters 2, hounds 3.
  • Maps are seeded: value-noise terrain from 5 dials (water, relief, cover, roads, warrens), set by 12 frames.
  • Warrens are dealt evenly across 4 kinds and rolled for depth, with roads along a minimum spanning tree between them.

The tick, in order.

  1. Your action.
  2. Growth or movement.
  3. Contact.
  4. The hunters sense, update the belief, then move (or retreat, in the last 3 ticks).
  5. Contact again, and siege status.
  6. Reinforcements: one every 11 ticks. Killed hunters respawn after 12.
  7. Dawn at tick 54.

The troop.

  • Growth in a warren: +1 a tick, rising to +2 once that warren is settled 10 ticks and +3 at 20. Nursery adds +1 more. A siege halts growth.
  • Specialty: attunes at +0.25 a tick toward the warren's kind and decays ×0.88 a tick otherwise.
  • Bonus by kind: bonus_k = 0.30 × max(in a k-warren ? 1 : 0, carried_k)
  • Strength:
    • size × (1 + C) in general;
    • defending a warren: × fortify(1.04, none in Scout) × (1 + D) × [walls 1.2];
    • attacking: × ambush(0.99) × [teeth 1.2].
  • Noise: √size × depth, where depth is deep 0.45, mid 0.7, shallow 1, multiplied by Combat ×1.44 or Nursery ×1.61. In the open: √size × terrain(open 1, road 1.4, forest 0.6, lake 1.2) × (1 − 0.3·S) × (holding ? 0.7 : 1).

Warren kinds trade off.

  • Combat: +30% strength, but loud.
  • Scout: shows you their belief map, but has thin walls (no fortify).
  • Defense: +30% when they come in, but slow to leave (−2 movement points on your first step).
  • Nursery: +1 extra person a tick, but loud.

Hunters.

  • Strength: 7.73 × kind × (1 + 0.287 × (night − 1))
  • Sweeper: ×1.0, sight 5, sweeps its own sector of the map.
  • Tracker (night 2+): ×0.9, sight 4, follows your tracks.
  • Listener (night 3+): ×0.8, sight 3, hearing ×1.9, unmuffled by forest.
  • Hound (night 4+): ×1.3, speed 3.
  • Numbers: 2 at dusk on night 1, +1 a night, plus the reinforcements.

The search AI: a shared Bayesian belief over all 504 cells.

  • The dusk prior: warrens only, weighted by your habits (×4 your share of ticks per warren) and by the family (×2 the line's share per kind).
  • Hearing, for each hunter each tick: a real roll against σ((r − d)/0.75), with r = 1.27 × noise × hear. Decoys add their own chance to be heard, and the hunter can't tell which it heard. Then the belief updates: b_i ×= heard ? p_i : 1 − 0.9 p_i.
  • Sight: Bresenham line of sight. Mountains block it; forest costs 2 sight per cell and 2 more if you're standing in it.
    • Seen: the belief collapses to your cell.
    • A decoy seen: it collapses to the decoy, the false lead.
    • Every empty open cell they can see: ×0.08. Warrens are never cleared by sight.
    • A warren mouth a hunter pokes into: ×0.05.
  • Motion model: 17% of each cell's mass diffuses to its neighbours, and 4% of open-ground mass pools back into warrens.
  • Targets: belief divided by distance, divided by crowding. Sweepers keep to their sectors; trackers follow fresh tracks (14 ticks).
  • Located means the belief at your cell is above 0.5.

Contact, the muster and siege: the rule that holds the game up.

  • In a warren they must be within 1 to contact you. In transit they must land on your square. Holding in the open, any contact is a fight.
  • Once you're located they muster at 3 blocks, and only go in when Σ group strength ≥ 1.01 × your defending strength.
  • Siege is being located with a hunter within 3. It halts growth, and the "watched" count runs.
  • The fight: mine × U(.75, 1.25) ≥ theirs × U(.75, 1.25). If you win, they all die and you lose 0.30 × theirs / per-person. If you lose, the run ends. Either way the fight is loud.
  • Attack: one hunter within 2 blocks.

Spending people (from the reserve first, then the troop; the troop never drops below 2).

  • Decoy −4, any time, one alive at a time: runs loud for the warren the hunters will take longest to reach, for 10 ticks.
  • Rearguard −5, any time, one at a time: fights the first hunter to reach it. If it wins, the hunter dies; if it falls, the hunter has lost 4 movement points. The fight is loud either way, so it's a false lead.
  • Scout −3, while stopped: shows their belief for 10 ticks.
  • Dig −8, only while holding, 3 or more squares from a warren: a shallow warren of your strongest attunement, or a Nursery if you have none.

Dawn and the line.

  • 33% of the troop wakes for the next night; the rest go to ground as the reserve.
  • The draft: dice deal 3 of 8 boons and you keep 1. The boons are brood, hush, teeth, walls, legs, dig deeper, dig and scatter.
  • The line: each run is a generation. When a run falls you pass on one heirloom per night survived, up to 3: an attunement (Combat-, Scout- or Defense-born) or a boon you held. A generation that dies on night 1 ends the line.

The machines.

  • Hashed, stateless dice: every roll is hash(seed, purpose, night, tick, id), so a twin replay changes only what it chose to change.
  • 11 invariants, all passing.
  • A worker-pool sweep runner, at about 1,300 games a second.
  • Dumb policies: always-stay, move-every-N, move-at-X, grow-to-X, attack-always and random, plus hybrids and a scout hybrid.
  • A lookahead expert, 14 ticks ahead on salted dice, with and without the new verbs.
  • A strict tuner judging against 17 written targets.
  • About a million games across tuning and evidence.

What they found, and what's still weak.

  • Siege-halts-growth is the dam. Turn it off and staying wins outright, at about 7 nights.
  • Attack earns its place, at about 0.9 nights to good play.
  • New hunter kinds are the strongest escalation.
  • Spending the family is now the skill that matters most (expert 2.94 → 4.29).
  • Transit speed is a cliff: at 2, running dies.
  • Weak points to attack:
    • the hunters know your exact size;
    • the skill ceiling is our own code;
    • "one tick earlier" is a wide window for good play, and a knife edge only for the greedy;
    • dig and hiding are barely used by the expert, about 1% of its time;
    • three heirlooms can add about half a night;
    • whether Warrens feel like different selves is still a human question.

3 · The standing brief

What it is. A tower defense game where you are the thing trying not to be found. You stay to grow, run or hold to hide, attack what's in reach, and spend your family to buy time. Then the line goes on. The feeling to design for: "I could stay three more ticks and get much stronger, but I'm gambling that they won't reach me." And then: "Fuck, I should have moved one tick earlier."

Interaction.

  • Space stays a tick (hold it to repeat). A attacks the weakest in reach. D R S G spend.
  • Click a warren to run, any square to hold, or a ringed hunter to attack. Hover anywhere for the race.
  • Transit plays itself out at 220ms a tick.
  • A run takes about 3 minutes, and most of its ticks are tense.
