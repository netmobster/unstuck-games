Brief for Claude Design

The Last Warren · Unstuck Games · 2 Oct 2026. The game in the Play tab is functionally complete and tuned. It was built function-first, as the Unstuck method says, and it's yours to make beautiful. This tab is everything you need to do that without breaking it.

What it is

A tower defense game where you are the thing trying not to be found. You're a troop hiding in a network of warrens. Every tick you gain people and every hunter moves one step. You choose to stay (grow, but they close in), run for another warren or go to any square and hold there (exposed in the open, hidden in forest), attack a hunter in reach, or spend people: a decoy, a rearguard, a scouting party, a new warren dug where you stand. Nights escalate. When your warren falls, a survivor carries one heirloom into the next generation of your line. When a generation fails to see its first dawn, the line ends: that was the last warren.

The feeling to design for: "I could stay three more ticks and get much stronger, but I'm gambling that they won't reach me." And then: "Fuck, I should have moved one tick earlier."

Tombstone: Tokyo Jungle (2012). We took its organ (where you settle decides who you become) and burned the rest: no animals, no city, no action combat, no unlocks. Please don't bring them back visually either. It's a burrow at night, not a nature documentary.

Mood

  • Night, underground, being hunted. Tension, not gore. Quiet until it isn't.
  • A survey map someone is reading by lamplight, which is where the current palette comes from. A full rethink is welcome as long as the meanings below survive.
  • Deadpan words. The prose is short and dry: "Seen." "The ring is forming." Keep that voice.
  • Embarrassingly simple was the prototype rule. Readable at a glance beats ornate. The board has to read in under a second at 14px cells.

What's fixed and what's yours

Fixed: please don't changeYours
  • The rules and every number. They're tuned on about 800,000 simulated games
  • The board grid (28×18 cells) and that the game is tick-by-tick
  • What the player can see, as listed under "Every state" below. The game is complete information, apart from the belief map, which only Scout warrens show
  • The ending is a sentence, not a score. Never a "62%", a star rating or a progress bar about how well someone did
  • "Show the working": the seed and replay stay one click away
  • All visual design: type, palette within the meanings, layout, the panels, the dialogs
  • Every sprite and tile (manifest below)
  • Motion: tick pacing, the transit animation, the siege ring, dawn
  • How the lineage and chronicle look (a family tree? a ledger? a burrow map?)
  • Mobile. It's desktop-only today and needs a real design, not a reflow

Screens

  1. Play. The board (a canvas) beside a side panel: the verbs, the troop, the hunters, the log, a legend. The header holds the line name, the night, the dawn bar and the tabs.
  2. Dawn draft (dialog). "Night N survived". The remnant line. Three boons; pick one.
  3. The warren fell (dialog). The ending sentence, then the twin line ("Running one tick earlier would have seen dawn."), then the heirloom pick: up to three, pick one. If the line ended, it says so and offers "Found a new line". "Show the working" sits below.
  4. Chronicle. The line's name, then each generation: name (for example Bramble III), nights, seed, the ending sentence, what it was born with and what it passed on.
  5. CD brief (this page). It can stay plain.

The side panel, as of v5

  • This warren: kind, gift (moss), cost (blood), and what the depth does to your noise. It's hidden when you're not in one.
  • What your size buys: a sentence such as "Holding here you'd beat 2 sweepers (64%), not 3 (31%)". The Attack button carries its own odds.
  • The reserve: at dawn, the ones who don't wake with you go to ground and sleep in the burrows. The reserve grows night after night, and spends draw on it first. It's the family, made visible.
  • Four spend buttons: Decoy −4, Rearguard −5, Scout −3, Dig here −8. They're greyed out when you can't afford one or the moment is wrong. These want icons, and they're the most new-to-the-player thing on the screen.

Interaction

  • Space stays one tick, and holding it keeps staying. A attacks the weakest hunter in reach. Click a warren to run for it, or any square to go there and hold; click a ringed hunter to attack. D R S G spend people. Hover anywhere to see the race.
  • Transit plays itself out at about 220ms a tick once you've committed. That's the "OH FUCK" stretch, so pace it on purpose.
  • A run takes about 3 minutes of real time, and around 70% of ticks are tense. There's almost no downtime to fill.

Questions for you

  1. Mobile: tabs, a bottom sheet, or something else? Three verbs and a board ought to fit a thumb.
  2. How do we make "Watched N ticks" legible on the board, not just in the panel? The longer they watch, the faster they react when you bolt.
  3. The line: should the chronicle look like a family tree, a ledger, or a burrow map?
  4. Do the warren kinds need their own visual language (Combat loud, Scout thin-walled, Defense slow to leave), so the trade-off is visible at a glance?
