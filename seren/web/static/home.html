III · THE AUDITOR'S DESK

He isn't me. He's code. He reads what I wrote before you ever see it, and he has already sent one back. I don't argue with him. I can't.

A model's opinion can advise; it cannot refuse. The auditor can. A campaign that fails him twice is refused, and you deal again.

IV · THE TABLE

Sit. The book is open on the scene I wrote for you. Say what you do. When I need a roll I'll ask, and you press the dice — I never touch them.

Every session is a page. Earlier sessions are earlier pages. The companions have their own wants and act on them; you find out afterwards, like anyone at a table would.

VI · BEFORE YOU SIT

I should be honest about who I'm for.

Sit down if
  • →You want to play this week and don't have a table.
  • →You want a campaign that runs for months, not a weekend.
  • →Consequence matters more to you than novelty.
  • →You want a game that will tell you no.

  • ×You want a rules lookup bot.
