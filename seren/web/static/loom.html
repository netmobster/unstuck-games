How the hand works

What this is

A hand of cards against Seren, and the winner is the campaign. You are not filling in a form — you are building a world out of the cards she deals you, and she keeps one card of her own.

What this is not

Not the game. Nothing here is played out — when the hand is laid it gets written up, and then Seren sits down and runs it. Nothing here is a settings screen either.

  1. She deals five of twelve. The other seven stay in her hand. A different five next time, which is the point — you build from the hand you were dealt.
  2. Click a card to read it, then play it. What it says under what it changes is what it does to the campaign, not flavour. Play a card again to take it back. Companions takes two.
  3. Name the trouble and she answers. She plays the antagonist herself, face down. Turn it over. Take it, or make her play another — but that slot is hers. You never hold her cards.
  4. Her three offers, top left. Deal these again is a fresh five for the slot you are on. New hand throws the whole table back and starts over. Play it out for me means she plays every slot and you watch.
  5. Play your loom when all seven are down. That does not build it yet — it takes you to the stakes.
  6. Tune it, then build it. Five dials, each one named. They never pick a card; they tell whoever writes the campaign how to write it — how absurd it may get, how big, how dangerous, whether trouble is a person or a place, how long it runs. Absurdity at the top does not choose the talking sheep. It makes one possible. Leaving every one alone is a perfectly good answer. Then: build my loom and play.
Tune your loom

These never pick a card. The hand is dealt and it stands. This is what the weaver is told about how to write it — and leaving every one of them alone is a perfectly good answer.
