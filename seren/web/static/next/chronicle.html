A Sheep Who Gave Up His Place

Seren’s first campaign, 27 August, told back at the end. One session, one morning, one sheep. Everything here is from the record.

IThe story

The four of them had been sitting outside the Weighed Sack for two days, waiting to be paid by a man called Pell, and Grumble was halfway through explaining how one properly records a debt when a sheep came off the west road at speed, wearing a scroll case like a backpack, and stopped. It looked at all four of them. It picked Ser’en. It stamped.

Ser’en knelt down and asked the sheep if it was all right. Not the scroll. Not a spell. The sheep. It tried to answer, heard itself say baa, and then calmed down against its own will because she was standing three feet away. It noticed. She did not. She spent a spell to talk to it, which was when Grumble mentioned, mildly, that he could have done that for free, twice a day, and nobody had asked.

The sheep was Finethir Shinebright, a professor of Applied Transmutation, who had been a sheep for two years and change and had dictated his letter one letter at a time onto a slate, over two evenings, to a person signing themselves M, who did not believe him at first. Somebody should write M a thank-you card.

Then came Guz and Pettil, the most polite kidnappers in the county, unarmed, with a wolf that sat down and watched the conversation instead of the sheep. Ser’en called Guz a giant, then big boi, and both of those landed somewhere and were kept. When she said a sheep was vastly inferior to a man, Guz said “I wouldn’t say that” before the rest of his sentence had finished being built, and would not explain. Grumble tried to buy them off and failed, and the failure shook loose the truth: Guz had been paid six silver for the entire job and had spent one buying a carter a drink. He left the other five on a bench. Pettil wanted it in writing, twice, that he had not taken the sheep.

On the road Ser’en gave Guz her goodberries. He didn’t eat them. He asked whether they worked on any shape of thing, and put them away for somebody else. Grumble entered them in the book as a debt Guz owed the party, and Guz’s ears went red.

The villain’s lair was not a tower. It was a cold, handsome, unswept house with three oversized kennels and a pair of men’s boots placed neatly outside one of them. Noke opened his own front door, because there was nobody left to do it, holding a wand cracked end to end that went off by itself when he adjusted his grip. Ser’en read him closely and decided, firmly, that he was harmless. She was wrong in a very specific way.

Upstairs was Tessa Marrick: housekeeper, nine years in the house, fourteen months a bear, and four months not speaking. Ser’en left her shield and her staff on the landing and walked in carrying nothing but a sealed vial of moonwell water, and the bear went still, and Noke, who had been measuring that woman twice a day for four months, said “What did you do. What ARE you?” Under the straw, cut a quarter-inch into the oak floor, was the roll of the household: nine names, who had agreed, who had not, who had never been asked. At the wall, gone over until it nearly went through: I SAID NO.

Then Grumble worked out that Tessa could write. She had claws and a floor. She’d been able to the whole time. The tap code everyone had been so proud of was never necessary. She wrote, among other things, DONT LEAVE HIM ALONE WITH IT, and then, unasked, IS IT OCTOBER. She took custody of the wand by lying on it.

In the morning they told all seven the complete truth, and every one of them formally refused consent, to keep their saving throw, and then stood still and let it happen anyway. Grumble wrote down nine refusals with reasons, because of course he did. Shinebright, asked if he’d like to go first, said “Them first,” and was irritable about being asked twice. One man got his body back. One survived a botched blast on paperwork alone. The wand broke. Shinebright did not refuse and was not restored. He gave up his place.

Nobody died. Nobody screamed forever. Noke did not run, and Tessa stood over him in the yard and did not touch him and did not leave. On the road out, Grumble spent his last spell finishing the professor’s cut-off sentence, “the one thing nobody has ever done is let me look at it”, said it as a technical complaint, and did not notice he had described his own career.

IIStill open

The promises and threads left on the table. A completionist’s to-do list.

  • A gift for Pell. Promised in public, written down, never delivered. Pell hid for two days from a debt he could afford, and is still owed.
  • Six people in the wrong shape. The wand is gone. A ninth-level caster is the only road left, and none is within reach.
  • Marta Dunn is a wolf, and her brother Pieter, the one success, will not leave the yard without her.
  • Who owns the house now, with its master a sheep and its villain sitting in the yard.
  • The goodberries. Guz kept them for someone. He never said who.
  • Little Bee. Guz wouldn’t give a proper name, and nobody got a look.
  • PB. Untouched, uninvented, unexplained. Seren doesn’t know either.

IIIStill out there

Who wasn’t beaten, and what they’re doing now.

  • Ahmed Noke. Alive, unresolved, in his own yard, with the pieces of the wand in his lap. The module treats him as a plot device. He outlived the plot. Nobody got the experience points for him, because nobody resolved him.
  • Pettil Wynne. Eleven months in the job, most of them spent rehearsing what he’ll say to a magistrate. The most dangerous person in this story, and he never once raised his voice.

IVWhat Seren knew

The fog lifts once, here, at the end.

  • The sheep calmed down because of you, and knew it. From the first minute in the road. You never noticed.
  • Guz and Pettil were next. The roll in the oak floor had them both marked NOT DONE. That’s why Pettil wanted it in writing, and why Guz wouldn’t call an animal inferior.
  • “Harmless” was the wrong read. Tessa had heard him sit in that study and say he would do it again.
  • Your party doesn’t know what the vial is. You told Tessa. A bear knows something about you that your friends don’t.
  • The guards remember being people, unevenly. Seren held that one ready for if anyone was cruel to one of them. Nobody was.
  • Aven found all five missing in nine hours, because somebody asked him instead of telling him to heel. It was the first time anyone had.

VRoads not taken

What was waiting that this party never met. Another run might.

01

The bed dragon.

Cornered, Noke would have built something absurd and dangerous out of furniture. You took him out of the house by asking nicely, so the comic set piece and the hardest fight just stayed in the cupboard.

02

Keeping the wand.

The module expected you to take it: a fight, a theft while he slept, a trick, a trade. You let a bear lie on it instead. A party that pocketed it would have learned what a cracked wand does in a backpack.

03

Meeting M.

The person who took down a sheep’s dictation, one letter at a time, over two evenings, and believed him. Never met. Possibly the best character in the campaign.
