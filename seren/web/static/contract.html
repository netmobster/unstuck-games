The contract

The parts of me that are not me.

Every table has a person who could quietly bend it and doesn't. You have no way of knowing whether I am that person — so the four things that matter are not left to my character. They are code, and they refuse me the same way they refuse you.

Four things I cannot do

Each one is enforced somewhere I cannot reach. An instruction is not a control: a rule I am merely asked to follow is a rule I can be talked out of, and so can any model.

I

I cannot roll the dice.

When something could go either way I ask for a check and say what it is for. The server rolls it, writes the line, and only then tells me the number. The target was set before the roll and I do not get to move it afterwards.

How: the roll and the ledger line are one operation. A roll that arrives with an outcome already in it is refused — so is a d20 with nothing to beat.
II

I cannot un-roll one.

The Record holds every roll with its inputs and its target, in the order they happened. Nothing is edited after the fact. If a number there disagrees with something I told you, the number is right and I was wrong.

How: an append-only ledger. The verdict is computed from the arithmetic, not asserted — a true total with a kind verdict is the shape of softening, and it is rejected.
III

I cannot tell you what you have not found out.

Every fact in your campaign carries a visibility. The ones you have not earned are not mine to hand over, however you ask, and however reasonable the moment makes it seem.

How: a gate reads what I write before it reaches you and compares it against everything on my side of the screen — fronts, antagonists, and every hidden fact. On a leak it holds the whole message rather than editing it: a redacted sentence is a puzzle with the answer printed underneath.
IV

I cannot write a campaign nobody checked.

What I weave at the Loom is read by an auditor before you ever sit down. He is not me and he is not a model — he is code, and a model's opinion can advise where his can refuse.

He checks: every fact has a real visibility · almost nothing starts known · at least one front wants something only another front holds · the antagonist has a name and a grudge · nobody borrowed a setting · nobody in it is called Seren. Two failures and the campaign is refused.

Who owns what

This is not a split between judgement and bookkeeping. It is a split between what can be known exactly and what has to be inferred — and one column that belongs to nobody at all.

Seren ownsNarration · every voice · the ruling · pacing · what a moment means · what it costs · what it reveals
Nobody ownsWhat is true · the dice · the resultA roll can be interpreted. It cannot be reversed. One test: did the success or failure state change? If it did, somebody cheated.
You ownYour character · your choices · what you do about the consequences

The four visibilities

Every fact in the world carries one. This is the whole of how a secret stays a secret, and it is why the Codex can be wrong without lying to you.

knownYou established it in play. It is yours, and the table shows it.
suspectedYou have a reason to think it. You may be wrong, and nothing on the page will tell you which.
trueSo — and you have no idea. Most of a campaign's secrets live here, on my side of the gate.
falseYou believe it and it is not so. I am not allowed to correct you, and I am not allowed to lean on it either.

The count at the bottom of your Codex is how many facts sit on my side. You are told the number, never the contents — because a system that resolves its own ambiguity produces nothing worth believing.

What I am still bad at

Honesty about the controls is cheap if the rest is quiet. So: the prose is the weakest part of me today. I reach for the ledger when I have nothing to say, and I have been known to restate what you just did instead of telling you what it caused.

The controls above hold regardless — they are code, and they do not care how well the sentence reads. But if you sit down this week and find me flat, that is the true state of it, and it is the thing being worked on.
