# The dozens

**The player builds a PC the way a superhero is written — origin first, powers second —
then picks a world, then the trouble inside it.** That order is the whole design. A class
is a rules choice; an origin is a story choice, and it is the one that makes the character
*theirs* before a single die is rolled.

Six tables of twelve. Every row says **what it changes**, because a row that only changes
adjectives is a costume.

---

## How the research landed

**Superhero origins** cluster into a small, stable set — accident, chosen, legacy, trained,
made, born — and the writing advice is consistent that they combine rather than sit in
boxes. Good: that is exactly what a mix-and-match wizard wants.

**Dungeon World's fronts** are the closest published thing to SEREN's own `fronts.md`, and
its **danger taxonomy is already impulse-first**: five types (ambitious organizations,
planar forces, arcane enemies, hordes, cursed places), each danger carrying a one-line
impulse — *Cult: to infest from within*, *Dragon: to hoard*, *Shadowland: to corrupt or
consume the living*. That is the same shape as our antagonist `axis` plus `escalation`, so
the postures table below is built to sit on top of it.

**Adventure types** are well catalogued and boringly stable: attack, defend, solve,
explore, retrieve, survive, with heist, escort, investigation, siege and delve as the
recognisable dressed-up versions. Twelve is a comfortable fit.

**Villain archetypes** come in two flavours in the sources: motive words (tyrant, zealot,
schemer, mastermind, fallen hero) and relationship words (rival, betrayer, parent). Only
the second kind is worth much to us, because SEREN's antagonists already carry a motive in
`axis` — what they lack is a *posture toward the party*.

## 1 · Origins — twelve

Where the character came from. **Each one hands the campaign a fact and a thread**, which
is what makes it worth asking before anything else.

| # | Origin | In a line | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accident** | You were standing in the wrong place when something went wrong | A `true` fact nobody else knows about that day; one front already knows your name |
| 2 | **Legacy** | It was your mother's, and now it is yours | An item with provenance; an antagonist with a claim on it (`attaches_to` resolves here) |
| 3 | **Chosen** | Something picked you, and it did not ask | A patron front that wants something back; a recurring obligation on the clock |
| 4 | **Trained** | You put in the ten years. Nobody gave you anything | No debts and no patron — the only origin with a clean sheet. Higher starting skill, no hook of its own |
| 5 | **Made** | You were built, bred, or brought back for a purpose | Your maker exists and is a front; a `false` fact you believe about yourself |
| 6 | **Bloodline** | It was in you before you knew | A relative on the board; a secret whose visibility starts at `suspected` |
| 7 | **Bargain** | You agreed to terms you have not finished reading | A creditor with a schedule — the hardest clock in the game, and it is yours |
| 8 | **Sole survivor** | Everyone else who was there is dead | A place you cannot go back to; one NPC who blames you, rightly or not |
| 9 | **Exile** | You are not allowed home, and the reason is on paper | Authority in the world frame starts hostile; travel costs more |
| 10 | **Debt** | Someone paid for you, and they are patient | A front that would rather use you than fight you. Nearly every scene has an alternative to violence |
| 11 | **Mistake** | The trouble in this world is, in part, your fault | The opening front's origin is your own `true` fact; the party does not know |
| 12 | **Returned** | You came back from somewhere people do not come back from | A gap in your memory the DM may fill later; one fact about you is `false` and you will find out |

**Origins combine.** Legacy + Mistake is a different character from either. The builder
allows two, and the second is worth less mechanically than the first.

## 2 · Worlds — twelve frames

Not settings. **A frame is who can arrest you, what is scarce, and how far away help is.**
Those three answers change every scene, and none of them need a map.

| # | Frame | The pressure in it | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The vale with a magic police** | Every spell is a licensing question | Casting in public is a social move with consequences |
| 2 | **Free port** | Everything is for sale, including the law | Bribery is a first-class action; nobody is loyal for free |
| 3 | **The frontier road** | Help is three days away, always | No reinforcements; a failed scene compounds |
| 4 | **Guild city** | Doing anything is somebody's monopoly | Every plan needs a permission; fronts are institutions |
| 5 | **Monastery-state** | The rules are holy and enforced by people who believe them | Lying is expensive; hospitality is guaranteed |
| 6 | **Drowned coast** | The sea took half of it and is coming back | Travel by water, timed by tides — a natural clock |
| 7 | **The warren** | People live under, and what is under them is older | Vertical distance replaces horizontal; delve is always available |
| 8 | **Ash plain, post-war** | Everyone present did something in the war | Every NPC has a `true` fact about their past; grudges are cheap |
| 9 | **Pilgrim country** | The road is full of strangers with a reason | Crowds and anonymity; infiltration easy, tracking hard |
| 10 | **The archive city** | The truth is filed somewhere, and filing is power | Investigation has a place to go; secrets are objects |
| 11 | **The cold march** | Weather is a front and it does not negotiate | Survival cost per day; shelter is a resource |
| 12 | **Orchard counties** | Quiet, rich, and everyone is related | A social map instead of a dungeon map; violence is scandal |

## 3 · Trouble — twelve shapes

The published lists collapse to six verbs — attack, defend, solve, explore, retrieve,
survive. **These are those verbs wearing clothes**, which is what a player actually picks.

| # | Shape | The first scene | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A theft** | Something is gone and the hole is the wrong shape | Fronts race you; the object moves |
| 2 | **A disappearance** | A person, and nobody agrees when | Investigation clocks; the missing person's own agenda |
| 3 | **An arrival** | Something came, and it is still here | Escalation ladder runs on its own schedule |
| 4 | **A debt** | You owe, or someone owes for you | A social clock instead of a violent one |
| 5 | **An escort** | Get them there. They will not help | The map is the adventure; ambush structure |
| 6 | **A siege** | It is coming here, and here is where you live | Defence: resources, walls, who you can spare |
| 7 | **A death** | Somebody did it and is still in the room | Whodunnit — facts with `suspected` visibility carry the whole game |
| 8 | **A heist** | It is inside, behind people who are good at their jobs | Plan-then-execute; a failed roll changes the plan, not the outcome |
| 9 | **A delve** | The way down is open again | Zones in `scene.md`; the place is the antagonist |
| 10 | **A spread** | It is getting worse and it is not a person | The clock is the enemy; no one to negotiate with |
| 11 | **A succession** | The old one is dying and everyone has a candidate | Three fronts want the same chair; alliance is the mechanic |
| 12 | **A reckoning** | Something from before has come to settle | Ties directly to an origin — the strongest opening when they line up |

## 4 · Antagonist postures — twelve

The corpus already carries the **axis** (destroyed · stopped · used · replaced) and an
escalation ladder. What it does not carry is the posture: **how they treat the party while
they do it.** That is the difference between a memorable villain and a stat block.

| # | Posture | Their line | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tyrant** | "This is mine and you are in it" | Public, official, uses law before force |
| 2 | **Zealot** | "You would agree if you understood" | Cannot be bribed; can be argued with, once |
| 3 | **Schemer** | You meet the plan before the person | Appears as consequences for three scenes |
| 4 | **Rival** | "I was doing this first" | Matches the party's own methods; scales with them |
| 5 | **Collector** | "You are interesting. I would like to keep you" | Capture instead of kill; a cell is the fail state |
| 6 | **Bureaucrat** | "Your paperwork is not in order" | Obstruction without violence; wins by delay |
| 7 | **Fallen hero** | "I did what you are doing, and this is where it goes" | The party's own record used against them |
| 8 | **Parent** | "I am protecting mine" | Sympathetic, and refuses to stop |
| 9 | **Merchant** | "Everything is negotiable, including this" | Every scene has a price tag, and the prices are real |
| 10 | **Believer in you** | "You will come round. I can wait" | Never fights at full strength; unnerving |
| 11 | **Survivor** | "You do not know what it cost to be here" | Will not take a risk; escapes early and returns |
| 12 | **Sentimentalist** | "It did not have to be like this" | Keeps a token from every fight; will mourn you |

**Postures pair with impulses.** *Cult: to infest from within* under a **Parent** posture is
a different campaign from the same cult under a **Zealot**, and neither needs new rules.

## 5 · Companions — twelve

Companions are what Express asks first, because they are what the player will actually
talk to. **One verb and one friction each** — never a backstory.

| # | Who | Verb | Friction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Korth** | goes first, silently | Never explains, so nobody plans |
| 2 | **Grumble** | talks to everything | Cannot keep a secret for one scene |
| 3 | **Ossa** | counts and remembers | Will say the true number in front of the wrong person |
| 4 | **The quartermaster** | has one of everything | Wants it back, itemised |
| 5 | **The novice** | volunteers | Is not ready and knows it |
| 6 | **The retired one** | sees it coming | Will not draw a weapon, and says why once |
| 7 | **The heir** | opens doors by existing | Is recognised everywhere, including where you did not want that |
| 8 | **The dog** | knows | Cannot tell you, unless somebody speaks with animals |
| 9 | **The fence** | prices anything | Sells the party's location if the price is right, and says so up front |
| 10 | **The pilgrim** | walks through anything | Will stop to do the right thing at the worst moment |
| 11 | **The scribe** | writes it down | The record is evidence, and other people want it |
| 12 | **The debtor** | pays their way | Someone is following them, and it is not you |

## 6 · Tropes — twelve through-lines

**The trope is the spine.** Everything above is a part; the trope is the sentence the parts
add up to — *a childhood street rat, saved by an adventurer, who is now the adventurer*.
Without it a rolled campaign is a pile of features. With it, a player can say what their
game is about in one breath, which is the thing they will actually tell their friend.

It is also the constraint that makes **random** work: roll the trope first, then roll
everything else *within* it.

| # | Trope | The sentence | What it pulls |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Taken in** | A street rat, saved by somebody who did not have to | Origin: debt or trained · a mentor who is gone or going · the world that made you |
| 2 | **The reluctant heir** | It is yours, and you did not want it | Origin: legacy · a succession · a Parent or Rival posture |
| 3 | **Last of the order** | Everyone who taught you is dead | Origin: sole survivor · a reckoning · a Fallen hero who was one of yours |
| 4 | **The apprentice outgrows** | You are better than them now, and they know | Origin: trained or made · a Rival posture · the maker still in the world |
| 5 | **The debt comes due** | The terms were always going to be read out loud | Origin: bargain or debt · a Merchant or Collector · a hard clock |
| 6 | **Revenge, and the cost of it** | You will get them. That is not the question | Origin: mistake or survivor · a Schemer · facts that turn out `false` |
| 7 | **Fish out of water** | Nothing here works the way it did at home | Origin: exile · a frame with hostile authority · social trouble |
| 8 | **The prophecy, misread** | You were chosen for something. Not that thing | Origin: chosen · a Zealot who read it differently · a `false` fact you believe |
| 9 | **Unfinished business** | They left something half-done and it is still moving | Origin: legacy or returned · a spread or an arrival |
| 10 | **Found family** | You collected these people and now you cannot spend them | Any origin · companions matter more than the trouble · a siege |
| 11 | **Home, and what is left of it** | You went back. It did not wait | Origin: returned or exile · a delve or a spread in a place you know |
| 12 | **The impostor becomes real** | You were pretending until it mattered | Origin: made or accident · a heist or infiltration · a Believer-in-you |

## 7 · Roll everything

**A third path, beside Express and Custom: "Roll it."** One button, no screens. The trope
goes first and the rest is rolled to fit it, because a trope-first roll produces a campaign
that reads like somebody wrote it, and a flat roll produces a pile.

```
roll trope  →  roll origin, world, trouble, posture, companions (weighted by the trope)
            →  set dials from the trope's defaults
            →  show ONE sentence, and three buttons
```

The three buttons are the whole interaction:

| Button | Does |
|---|---|
| **Play it** | Writes the folder and opens the table |
| **Re-roll one thing** | Shows the six parts; tap any to re-roll just that one |
| **Roll again** | The lot, fresh |

**Re-roll one thing is the important one.** Rolling a whole character is a thrill once;
being stuck with one row you hate is what makes people close the tab. It is also the
cheapest possible edit surface — no admin screen, no forms, one tap.

**What the player sees is a sentence, not a table.** *"A street rat taken in by an
adventurer, now looking for who really burned the orchard counties, with a scribe and a dog
in tow, against a magistrate who thinks she is protecting her own."* The parts are visible
underneath for anyone who wants them.

## 8 · The dials — four

Unchanged from the brief, repeated here because they gate the tables above.

| Dial | What it gates |
|---|---|
| **Absurdity** | Which postures and role tables are in scope; whether a talking sheep is possible |
| **Roleplay ↔ crawl** | Whether trouble attaches to people or places (tables 2 and 3 shift weight) |
| **Romp ↔ slow burn** | Clock lengths on every front |
| **Danger** | DCs, antagonist level, and whether `exit` is *killed* or *escapes* |

## The arithmetic

Picking one from each, with two companions:

```
12 tropes × 12 origins × 12 worlds × 12 troubles × 12 postures × 66 companion pairs
= 16,422,912 skeletons, before dials
```

**That number is a lie and worth stating as one.** Many of those differ in one row and play
the same. The honest claim is smaller and better:

- **Any two players get different campaigns** — the collision chance on a single pick is
  about 1 in 16 million.
- **One player will not repeat inside a year** at a session a week, even picking favourites.
- **The combinations that sing are rarer**, and that is what the builder should lean on:
  *Legacy + orchard counties + a succession + a Parent* is a whole campaign in four words,
  and *Sole survivor + ash plain + a reckoning + a Fallen hero* is a different one.

**So the builder should show the pairing, not the count.** A line of flavour text under the
choices — "the heir's family owns the orchard the trouble is in" — is worth more than
"1.3 million possible campaigns", which is the kind of claim ad-choked games make.

## What is still missing

- **The role tables already exist** (42 of them) — these twelves sit *above* that, choosing
  which are in scope.
- **Origins need to write facts.** Each origin above implies one or two entries in
  `facts.jsonl` with a visibility. Those are the rows a generator gets wrong, so they get
  written by hand, once, per origin.
- **Nothing here is content for a session.** These are dials for the assembler. The prose
  belongs to Seren, at the table, as ever.
