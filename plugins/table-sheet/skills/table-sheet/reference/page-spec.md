# The page

One scrolling page with a sticky jump bar. Every section earns its place by
answering a question the player has mid-fight.

## Order, and what each section is for

1. **Header.** Name, class, level, campaign, and a strip of the numbers they get
   asked for out loud: AC, HP, initiative, spell save DC, spell attack, the one
   or two saves that matter, passive Perception, speed. Say when the numbers
   were pulled.
2. **Their role, in one honest paragraph.** What this character is for, and what
   they can do when the plan fails. Back it with the at-will damage or healing
   number so it is not a pep talk.
3. **What fits in one turn.** Movement, action, bonus action, reaction, plus
   whatever persists across rounds. List what this character can actually put in
   each. Include the rules that bite: one spell slot per turn, one
   concentration spell, a reaction called before the roll.
4. **The engine.** The signature resource, explained properly: what it costs,
   how long it lasts, what the options are, when to switch. A short sequence
   (cast it, what happens on their turns, what it costs to move it) beats a
   paragraph.
5. **Fight playbooks.** Four to six named situations — the opening round, one
   big enemy, a crowd, the party is losing, they come for you, before anyone
   rolls — each a short ordered list. This is the section people actually use.
6. **Every spell and feature, in plain English.** Grouped by level. Each card:
   name, casting time, range, concentration, the numbers, one sentence on what
   it does, one on when to use it. Mark what is free, what is always prepared,
   what is new this session. A filter row (prepared / concentration / bonus
   action / support / damage) pays for itself on a phone.
7. **Kit and skills.** Magic items with what they actually do, limited-use
   features with their counts, the skills worth rolling and the passives.
8. **Playing them.** Built from the player's own personality, ideals, bonds,
   flaws and backstory. What they sound like, what they will and won't do, their
   tell. A short table of "this buff, described as the character would do it"
   turns mechanics into roleplay.
9. **Before next session.** A checkbox list: what to fix on the sheet, what to
   ask the DM, what was settled last time. Ticks can live in browser storage.

## What makes it good

- **Numbers, not adjectives.** "About 30 damage a round with no slots," not
  "solid damage."
- **Averages next to dice.** `3d8+4 · avg 17.5`.
- **Say when something does not work.** Call Lightning needs open sky; Commune
  with Nature does nothing in a city. A page that only lists strengths gets a
  player killed.
- **One idea per line.** They are reading this in the dark.
- **Their language.** If the player calls their character a bubbler, the page
  says bubbler.

## Craft

Phone-first: one column under 400px, 16px gutters, nothing wider than the
screen. Light and dark both, with colours defined as tokens on `:root` and
redefined for dark — never a colour that only exists in one theme. Tabular
numerals wherever dice line up. A display face with personality for headings, a
highly legible face for body, a mono for dice. Pull the palette from the
character's own world rather than a default: moonlight on water reads
differently from a Battle Master's steel and blood.

Do not put the character's story in a hero image. Put it in the writing.
