The promise

What the teardown said was alive

Mendel's organ: you do not control the creature. You change the world it is in, and it responds in its own time.

Galápagos gave its creature a will and gave the player only the environment — you rearrange the room and Mendel walks through it, occasionally into something fatal. The game was buried by its platform and its difficulty, but the organ was intact: a creature with its own agenda, and a player reduced to conditions.

That was the promise we took into the build. What follows is what survived contact.

That reaction is the entire design validated in five words, and it arrived before any art, sound or polish existed. The hoard was not in the brief. It emerged because stolen things persisted between runs and the sim had nowhere to put them but under the couch.

The transplant

What we kept, and what we cut

KEPT · the organ itself

You never steer Lucy. You pack a cage, open a door, and get out of the way. Every verb the player has is a condition, not a command.

KEPT · the world as the interface

Mendel's rooms became a seven-room house with real doors, real objects and real routes. The house is the control panel.

KEPT · consequence without punishment

Nothing kills Lucy. The cost of a bad idea is a wasted day and a grumpy ferret, which turned out to be enough.

CUT · difficulty as the teacher

Galápagos taught by killing you. Jay's boundary — "more adorable and weird than chaos and danger" — removed death entirely, and the game got more interesting, not less.

CUT · the puzzle

Mendel is a puzzle game with an autonomous piece. Ferret Bowling has no solution, and refuses a score. The payoff is the sentence at the end of the run.

CUT · the launcher

The genre DNA promised throwing an animal for distance. Lucy cannot be aimed, and the title kept the joke: Bowling Optional Edition.

What the build discovered

Four things nobody designed

1 · Moods are attention, not stats. The items you pack do not make Lucy stronger. They change what she notices — a tag multiplier on the world. The same house produces a different day because she is looking at different things. This is now the cleanest idea in the library.

2 · The hoard is the retention hook. Treasures persist under the couch between runs. Nothing else in the game makes a player say "one more run" — not the journal, not the combos.

3 · Habits make a specific ferret. She develops a favourite nap spot, a favourite room, a nemesis and a routine, each decaying if unused. When a mood meets a habit you get a ritual — the shoe nest, the pancake on her spot, the crumbs in the treasure pile. Persistence is what turns a simulation into a character.

4 · Leftover mood across runs. Deciding to play with her does not mean she is in the mood for it. Ten to twenty per cent of yesterday's temper survives into today — hyper, sleepy or grudgy — and a bath washes it away. That single rule is what stopped runs feeling independent of each other.

None of those four were in the brief. All four came out of testing, and three came out of Jay's own asides during a decision round.

Honestly

What is still wrong with it

  • No sound. The brief exists — a room bed, seven Lucy sounds, eight event sounds, four moments — and it is Jay's next job.
  • Labels collide in the living room when she drags things around. Known, unfixed, visible.
  • One seed of content. The journal phrasing repeats sooner than it should. This is where pre-generated variety earns its place, the way Bad Monkeys does it.
  • Art is the red team's, not CD's finals. Good enough to ship an alpha, not good enough to ship.
  • Mobile is untested for the prototype specifically.

All of that is in the alpha notice the player sees before the first run, which says the quiet part: some of that is a bug, some of that is Lucy, telling them apart is genuinely hard.

The thread

Three games in, one verb

Orbis: conditions, never interference. Elsewhere: your absence is the mechanic. Bad Monkeys: she works while you are gone. Ferret Bowling: you cannot aim the ferret. Four games, one verb — you do not control the thing, you set up conditions and find out.

That is either an identity or a rut, and naming it is what chose game #4. The next tombstone is Majesty (2000), whose organ is the same refusal seen from the other side: you cannot give an order, only an offer, and the offer can be declined. The difference is that this time you are present, paying, and out of time.

The ferret taught us that persistence makes a character. The next one has to prove that twelve of them can.
