**Their reframe:** not "Paperclips with a story" but **"a game about what happens to a person and their machine when you aren't there."**
- Paperclips supplies the structural trick: each layer makes the previous way of thinking obsolete.
- Elsewhere supplies absence as simulation.
- SEREN supplies generated structured possibility.
- Orbis supplies determinism and a measurable state space.
- Bad Monkeys supplies what none of those can: **Jame.**

**What they like**
- **"Not always better, always weirder" is the emotional contract.** If absence produces optimization, it's an idle game; if absence produces *personality*, it's something stranger.
- **The last sentence is an input to the simulation, not flavour text.** (Example: "Keep your head down" read literally, something important traded away, a bizarre part acquired because that's what "head down" meant to her.)
- **The one-sentence ending is right.** Paperclips escalates until the machine becomes the subject; this is the opposite: *the machine accumulates a person.* Resist adding a giant final reveal; the sentence is the artifact.

**Biggest concern: content soup.** Generation + dice can produce 10,000 amusing nouns that aren't combinatorially meaningful. Generated records must participate in the mechanical grammar (object, tags, upside/downside, solves, personality, persistence). The generator should manufacture **new pieces of the game's language**, and deterministic seeds let us torture-test that.

**Protect this: Jame must be able to fuck up.** Not fail the game; fuck up. If absence always produces an optimal or satisfying result, players learn leaving her alone is the correct strategy. The emotional engine is the oscillation between *"what did you do while I was gone?"* and *"…actually, that's brilliant."*

**Their loop:** you give a goal + one sentence → she interprets it → the system rolls the world → you return to consequences → you decide whether to keep them.

**Name:** **NOT BETSY is the strongest title by a mile**, because it makes you want to know why she's called that, and once you know, the title is the game's first joke. **BAD MONKEYS is the universe. NOT BETSY is the game.** Ship that distinction.
