The 2024 rule that catches people: you can spend one spell slot per turn. Healing Word and Moonbeam can't both happen on the same turn — but a slot spell plus a cantrip, or plus one of her free casts, is fine.

The engine: Starry Form

One Wild Shape use and a bonus action buys ten minutes of starlight. She can still cast in it, unlike a beast shape — and from level 10 she can change constellation at the start of every turn.

Archer

Chalice

Moonbeam, the spell people underuse

One action, one 2nd-level slot, and a pillar of moonlight lands within 120 ft. Anyone who ends a turn in it or walks into it takes 2d10 radiant on a failed CON save — and that costs her nothing more. Moving it costs her whole action, so she puts it where enemies have to be.

Upcast, it scales hard: 3d10 at 3rd, 4d10 at 4th, all the way to 6d10 — and every trigger for the full minute uses that number.

It also forces shapeshifters back into their true form, which makes it an answer to things wearing a face.

Playing her

She listens more than she speaks, and what she says lands harder for it.

From her own sheet

Ideal. The land holds memory. What happened somewhere matters even when no one else remembers it.

Bond. Her moonpool is gone. She carries the last of it in a vial she hasn't opened.

Flaw. She doesn't ask for help — not from pride, she genuinely doesn't notice she needs it until it's late.

How that plays at the table

Describe buffs as gifts. Give the effect, not the mechanic: "you feel ready," not "+1d4 to your next save."

Let someone else have the moment. The reward she plays for is a friend noticing out loud that her help made their turn work.

She thinks of what she does as maintenance, not power — so she'll undersell the round where she saved everyone.
