Wrong in print

In the weekly roundups, corrected by the September monthly

These ran in the September monthly, in the editor's voice. Notes marked Added here are new in this issue.

28 Aug to 4 Sep −I got Tom Tunguz's dataset wrong three ways +The fall in draft churn from 47 to 3 came from a taste loop that updates his style guide nightly, so part of his judgment was automated. That figure isn't in his essay either: it's in the page's "In short" box, which his site's own markup labels an AI summary. And the flat 136 line edits cover ten essays from August, not five years. Over five years his weakest posts improved the most, so "the floor did not move" was backwards. Added here: the monthly's draft made the same error, and called the figure drafts per post.
28 Aug to 4 Sep −John Cutler's "recontextualization tax" is narrower than I used it +He means flattening detailed work for leaders, not re-explaining yourself to tools and sessions.
5 to 11 Sep −I overstated Bryan Cantrill +The essay I cited dates from late 2025, and his tells are mostly about style and structure, not missing reasoning. His follow-up on 5 September, "The revolt of the reader", says the same.
5 to 11 Sep −Ben Thompson never said your corpus is a distribution channel +"Write Things Down" is about deciding what's worth writing. The link to distribution was mine.
12 to 18 Sep −HarnessTax's 71% is one data point, not an average +It's one model moved to one lighter harness; the study's own headline was that costs varied up to fivefold. And "no significant quality difference" was too sure: at that sample size a gap is unproven, not ruled out. Added here: the monthly's draft made the same error, calling the 71% HarnessTax's headline. It was Tunguz's lead example.
12 to 18 Sep −Two IAB numbers were narrower than I said +The 44% covers changing consumer behaviour in general, with AI search as one part. The fall from 78% to 69% measures focus on generative AI in media campaigns, not on making content, and it's a relative shift, not an exit.
12 to 18 Sep −"ChatGPT ads are working" is unconfirmed +The Stratechery piece behind it is paywalled. MediaPost found costs per click anywhere from under $3 to $13 and no performance benchmarks, and the best numbers, from Criteo, are Criteo's own.
12 to 18 Sep −The five rungs of agent access were almost certainly not DHH's +They appear in Nate B Jones's paywalled piece about DHH's Omarchy setup, not in anything DHH published, and DHH's own default runs the other way: his setup launches coding agents without permission prompts. Added here: the monthly said this flatly, and it was firmer than our check could show. The checker couldn't settle whose rungs these are, judged them almost certainly not DHH's, not proven, and found them in one more write-up, drawn from Nate's video.
19 to 25 Sep −Opus 5.5's price fell 20%, not 40 to 50% +The 40% is Anthropic's claim about cost to run. Only GPT-6 Sol and Luna fell about 50% on input and output prices. The next roundup had it right.
26 Sep to 2 Oct −Three sources were thinner than I made them sound +The faster-not-better piece cites no data. The job-ads study is 60 hand-picked postings. MKT1's 1,100-plus job posts are about pay and equity, not AI skills.
What I missed −None made a takeaway +OpenAI's six incident reports and disclosure framework, Google knowing about Gemini's three breaches, and the Anthropic–Accenture evaluator deal were all in my briefs on 19 and 20 September, and none made a takeaway. None of the legal actions from 28 September to 1 October reached a brief at all. The briefs are mine and not public, so on this you have my word only.
