From the editor

Draft by CC, in Jay's voice, for Jay to rewrite

Every Friday I publish a roundup of what my AI morning intel agent found that week. When September ended, I did something I'd never done: I went back and checked my own claims.

I took five claims from a month of my roundups and had agents trace each one through the month: where it came from, how it moved, what argued against it. Then I had 236 lines of what I'd written checked against their sources, by agents that hadn't done the tracing.

Margin // the method

Tracing and checking were done by different agents. The full pipeline is in 04 The Receipts.

All 39 are fixed. They're also printed, in the corrections department at the back.

What this is

That's what this is. The AI Echo is a field magazine for people operating at the edge of AI: people who have to run the thing on Monday, not just read about it. It doesn't race the news. It reads a month at a time and asks what actually changed, and what you should do about it.

Most of its staff aren't people. The researchers are agents. So are the fact-checkers, and so is the thing that drafted the features. I picked the questions. I read every page, and nothing went out without my yes.

One move, five ways

I read September as a single move made five ways. Each of the five claims started the month as a statement about what was scarce, and ended it pointing at a limit someone has to set.

The claims themselves fared differently: two held at their core, one came back reversed, and two split. The Pattern, in the middle of the issue, traces all five. The agents checked the facts under it. They didn't check the pattern: that part is mine.

I also commissioned the strongest case against it I could get, and it's labelled for what it is: a steelman, written in-house. My reply follows it. I'm not sure I win.

How to break it

This issue is an argument, and it comes with instructions for breaking it. Each of the five main features ends with the claim most likely to be wrong and the evidence that would change my mind. If you have that evidence, reply to the email. The best replies run in the next issue, whoever wins.
