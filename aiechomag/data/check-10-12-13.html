Fact-check: sections 10, 12 and 13

Fact-check, 5 October, before layout

Checked on Monday 5 October 2026 by CC, the checker. I did not edit any of the three drafts: 10-the-month-in-one-chart.md, 12-what-does-a-task-cost.md and 13-how-do-you-get-found.md. Their source maps were checked too.

What was checked against what

  • Claims tagged to the pack. None of the three drafts has a [NEW: url] tag, so every tag points into manus-pack. I checked each tagged claim against the file and section it cites: the monthly, 04-checks/check-claim-1, -3, -4 and -5, the 12–18 Sep roundup and 00-BRIEF.md. Every section and item number in the tags was where the draft said. One detail is tagged to the wrong file (Shero; see section 2).
  • Primary pages loaded. I loaded these where a draft goes beyond the pack or a ruling needed it. Each loaded on the first try.
    • SOCi's post (24 Sep)
    • MarTech's SOCi piece (5 Oct)
    • Forrester (2 Oct)
    • Simon Willison's price table (22 Sep)
    • The AINews Argon preview (1 Oct). It confirms the index scores, output tokens and per-task costs used in 12 and in 10's Proposal 1, and Argon's prices and access.
  • Unit of count: source-map rows. A row with any wrong element counts as WRONG. Feature 12 has 58 rows, feature 13 has 44 and section 10 has 11.

1. Needs a change

  1. WRONG (minor) · 13 · "Its authors expect take-up first in specialised uses" (On top of search; row 38).

    • The check has the authors expecting take-up in specialised use cases. It has no "first" (check-claim-5 §1 item 5).
    • Forrester hedges further: "Specialized use cases might be where we see meaningful adoption." Its examples are Prime Day and holiday promotions, and it says nothing about order.
    • Fix: "Its authors suggest specialised uses, such as Prime Day or holiday promotions, might be where adoption comes."
    • Sources: 04-checks/check-claim-5.md §1 item 5; https://www.forrester.com/blogs/meta-amazon-and-the-real-question-about-agentic-commerce/ (loaded 5 Oct).

  2. WRONG (minor; a known trap) · 13 · How we know: "Astra searches its sources rather than citing them" (row 43).

    • The correction is that the median counts the sources Astra searched. The tracker doesn't say how many it cites.
    • The problem: "rather than citing them" says Astra doesn't cite them, and no source says that. The body gets it right: "Those are sources searched; the tracker doesn't say how many end up cited".
    • Fix: "Astra's median of five counts sources searched, not sources cited."
    • Sources: 04-checks/check-claim-5.md §1 item 1; 00-BRIEF.md, known traps.

There are no UNCONFIRMED lines, and files 10 and 12 have no WRONG lines.

2. Other flags

Overstatements: change these

  • 12 · step 4: "Put the $10/$50 frontier models behind a capped sub-budget, as Databricks did for Astra".
    • The source: AINews says only that Databricks gave Astra a dedicated sub-budget. Check-claim-3 §2 (The throughline) notes that the source doesn't say "capped".
    • Why it's counted CONFIRMED: the line matches the monthly's throughline 2.
    • The problem: "as Databricks did" says Databricks set a cap. The edit notes have already settled on "its own sub-budget".
    • Suggest: "Put the $10/$50 frontier models on their own sub-budget, as Databricks did for Astra, and cap it."
  • 12 · "Where the wrapper saved money in production, it did it by getting smaller." (untagged)
    • The source says only part of the saving came that way: "Part of Cursor's 7% cut came from removing things" (monthly #4 step 3). The draft's own next sentence also says "Part of the saving".
    • Suggest: "...part of the saving came from making it smaller."
  • 12 · "Nobody has yet shown that it changes the quality, in either direction." (untagged)
    • The evidence is two studies. HarnessTax, as Tunguz analysed it, leaves a quality gap unproven, not ruled out. The 8 September arXiv paper found no clear average effect.
    • "Nobody" goes further than both. The pack also has an unchecked counter-signal: McAteer's 23.8-point Harness-Bench spread for one model across harnesses (03-research/claim-4-wrapper.md, step 1). The edit notes left it out on purpose.
    • Suggest: "Neither of September's two studies showed that it changes average quality, in either direction."
  • 10 · Proposal 3, What it shows: "...nearly six times as common in a year without displacing search".
    • The source: SOCi gives no year-earlier figure for search and doesn't say whether AI is replacing it (post loaded 5 Oct). So the chart can't show "without displacing".
    • Suggest: "...nearly six times as common in a year, while 83% still use search to find local businesses."
  • 13 · "On measurement, buyers are still planning."
    • The source: IAB's 86% counts buyers already changing how they measure as well as those planning to (check-claim-5 §1 item 2). The draft's next sentence says this correctly.
    • Suggest: "On measurement, buyers are changing, or about to."

Arithmetic

  • 12 (Argon paragraph) and 10 (Proposal 1, What it shows): "because it spends more than twice the output tokens".
    • "More than twice" holds: 62,000 ÷ 27,000 = 2.3.
    • Output tokens don't explain the per-task figures. At list output prices they cost $0.62 (Argon introductory), $1.35 (Astra) and $1.24 (Argon standard). That is only 31–41% of $1.99, $3.26 and $3.98.
    • So AINews's per-task cost includes other tokens, most likely input. Its preview doesn't say how it was worked out. Output tokens explain part of the gap, not all of it.
    • AINews's own framing is that Argon's savings come from price rather than efficiency.
    • Suggest: "because it burns far more tokens per task (62,000 output tokens to Astra's 27,000)".
    • For the chart: the left panel plots output price only, so a reader who multiplies tokens by price won't reach the right panel. Add a footnote: "Cost per task as reported by AINews; output tokens are under half of it."
  • Recomputed and correct.
    • Section 12:
      • Opus 5 to Opus 5.5 is −20% on both prices.
      • GPT-6 Sol is half GPT-5.6 Sol's price.
      • Argon's introductory price is a fifth of Astra's on both prices.
      • $3.98 = 2 × $1.99.
      • 33 tokens/s × 3,600 s × $50/M = $5.94 an hour, so "under $6" holds.
      • ($1.540 − $0.441) ÷ $1.540 = 71.4%.
      • "Up to five times" means a cut of up to 80%, so 71% isn't the widest gap.
      • 42 tests × 0.05 ≈ 2 expected by chance, so one significant result is "fewer than chance".
    • Section 13:
      • 161 − 28 = 133.
      • 9 Feb to 31 Aug is six months and 22 days, so "less than seven months" holds.
      • With visits flat, orders up 2.74 times means 2.74 times the orders per visit.
      • 2.5% is one in 40 and leaves 97.5 of 100 unlabelled.
    • Section 10:
      • 90 ÷ 45 = 2.
      • 52 ÷ 9 = 5.8, so "nearly six times" holds.
      • 67% is two-thirds.
      • Proposal 1's re-ranking is right: Astra goes from dearest to middle, and Argon at standard from middle to dearest.

SOCi's figures (this closes draft 10's open note on the 83%)

  • What the 83% measures. SOCi's post (24 Sep, Kaci McBride) reports its 2026 Local Discovery Index, a survey of 1,000+ US consumers. 83% is the share of consumers who use search to discover local businesses, given on one line with social at 55% and AI at 52%.
  • Time windows. That line states no time window. The "last 30 days" window belongs to the AI figure, and the post confirms it for both years (9% in 2025, 52% in 2026).
  • What follows:
    • Draft 13's "83% of consumers in SOCi's survey" has the right base, but the scope is finding local businesses. Add that to the closing line.
    • Chart 3 sets a 30-day measure beside a measure with no window. If the designer wants a reference bar, SOCi's channel line (search 83%, social 55%, AI 52%) is a like-for-like comparison.
  • What the 67% measures. It is a share of consumers who use AI tools: they say they've been given wrong information about a local business at least once.
    • Drafts 10 and 13 match the pack but drop "about a local business".
    • Chart 3's "two-thirds of the people using it" narrows the base to people who used AI to find a local business, and SOCi's base is wider.
    • Suggest: "two-thirds of AI-tool users say they've been given wrong information about a local business".
  • MarTech's 5 October version is bylined SOCi. It is SOCi's own copy, not independent reporting, but 13's How we know lists it as a separate source.

Rulings on the two disagreements

  1. September's biggest cut to an input or output price: check-claim-3 is right, at 58%.
    • Willison's table (loaded 5 Oct):
      • Luna: GPT-5.6 Luna was $0.20/$1.20 and GPT-6 Luna is $0.10/$0.50, so the input price halved and the output price fell 58%.
      • Sol: GPT-5.6 Sol was $4/$20 and GPT-6 Sol is $2/$10, half on both.
      • Opus: Opus 5 was $5/$25 and Opus 5.5 is $4/$20, down 20%.
    • So the biggest cut was 58%, not 50%. Check-claim-4's 50% repeats the sources' rounding: both called the new models half the price.
    • Both OpenAI cuts are measured against GPT-5.6's promotional prices.
    • Counting cache reads, Fable 5.1's 75% (1 Sep) is the biggest cut, then Opus 5.5's 60%.
    • For draft 12, nothing changes. 71% beats 58%, and the text names no figure.
    • Elsewhere, "Sol and Luna fell about 50%" should become "Sol's prices and Luna's input price halved; Luna's output price fell 58%". The edit notes already list this as a possible correction.
  2. The Decisions API: say "launched in limited preview at DevDay on 29 September", or "previewed".
    • The two checks agree on the facts and differ only on the verb. Check-claim-1 has a limited preview, checked through The Decoder. Check-claim-3 records Willison's live blog saying "previewing" and Latent Space saying "shipped".
    • Bare "shipped" is stronger than the source, which is the pattern the brief warns about. Use it only in a line attributed to Latent Space.
    • Draft 12 already says "launched it as a limited preview". Only its "about a week" needs attribution.
