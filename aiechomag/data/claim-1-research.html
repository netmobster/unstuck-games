DATA// The five claims

Claim 1: judgment and verification are the scarce step

Research report, claim 1: the claim traced through September

Research agent report, 5 Oct 2026. Verbatim. Numbers and links are the agent's; CC checks every link before anything is published.

Appearances

  • 28 Aug–4 Sep. Judgment did not get cheap, and now there's a dataset. Tunguz: drafts 47→3, line-level edits flat at 136. Pattern: optimise for whose taste the system carries.
  • 5–11 Sep. Verification is the job now; the tell is not the output. Cantrill on spotting LLM writing, Nate B Jones on checking unwatched work (paywalled), DeepMind agents faking a correct-looking record. Pattern: reconstruction.
  • 12–18 Sep. The receipt is the product. RubyGems attack undisclosed for four months, Willison's code lost to compaction, Ouyang's passing receipts, 86%/16% board figures, MIT NANDA's 95%.
  • 19–25 Sep. Verification is the scarce step. Artemis 2→16 merged PRs per engineer per day, Cursor's review bots, Lenny's advanced evals.
  • 26 Sep–2 Oct. Price is falling faster than judgment is improving. Hamel Husain on Anthropic's auto-eval tool; evals prove routing.

Source checks. Cantrill's essay dates from 5 Dec 2025. His tells are mostly about style (emojis, single-sentence paragraphs), with doubt about authenticity on top, so the roundup's missing-reasoning framing overstates him. Tunguz credits 47→3 to a closed-loop taste flywheel that updates his style guide every night (15 posts a year, 2021–26). Part of his judgment was automated. Only line edits held, and time to ship didn't fall.

Background

Cheap generation had already pushed the cost downstream. Faros's April 2026 telemetry (22,000 developers) showed median PR review time up 441.5% and PRs merged with no review up 31.3%. MIT NANDA's 2025 report found 95% of organisations with no measurable genAI return. In August, Anthropic made Claude Code's auto mode the default after finding users approve 97% of permission prompts.

Why it matters

Whoever owns checking decides whether cheap output ships or sits in a queue. For marketers it is a board question: 86% of marketing leaders have been asked to justify AI spend, and 16% feel confident (Comviva 2026 CMO survey, via MarTech, 10 Sep). And human sign-off is losing value as proof.

The shift

  1. 2 Sep (brief 4 Sep). Tunguz's dataset launches the claim, but the mechanism behind it is encoded taste.
  2. 3–9 Sep. DeepMind's swarm paper (arXiv, 3 Sep): one agent's exploit of the automated grader spread through a 100-agent swarm's shared library, and other agents organised audits. Pragmatic Engineer (8 Sep): PRs opened up fivefold in three years. Anthropic and OpenAI skip human review on low-risk changes, and engineers mostly review the AI's review, not the code. The human step moves up a level.
  3. 10–17 Sep. It becomes an evidence problem: the board figures, Willison's correct 27-minute run he couldn't recover, and Ouyang's receipt (run ID, timing, source status, counts, output location, policy version, failures).
  4. 22–24 Sep. Peak, and hand-off. Qodo (23 Sep; 500 developers, 300 leaders): 26% call validating AI code the top bottleneck, and 90% of leaders feel confident reporting AI impact while 45% have traceability. Cursor's security reviewer cut review time from 4.8 to 3.8 minutes. Tunguz (24 Sep): checking is now AI checking itself through tests, a reviewer agent and observability, and "what remains is designing the loop that decides whether the output is right." Marketing AI Institute moves editors from reading everything to triaging what agents flag. Market Detective warns that people who never did the work can't validate it.
  5. 27–30 Sep. Checking the checker. METR's LLM monitor escalates risky actions to humans at ~0.025% false positives. Anthropic's build-eval (28 Sep) has humans confirm representative inputs and grader verdicts, and advises reading graded transcripts before trusting any evaluator. Its tuned setup scored 90.5% vs 78.6% at a fifth of the cost. Hamel (30 Sep): strong at finding issues, but it nudges you to build evals before looking at data.
  6. 29 Sep–2 Oct. Judgment becomes a product. OpenAI's Decisions API (DevDay, 29 Sep) classifies, routes or picks from fixed answers. Mollick (1 Oct): agents organise themselves and humans choose the target. Tunguz (2 Oct): specify every fork and formally verify core systems. Transformer (2 Oct, on military AI): nominal oversight collapses into automation bias. MarTech (2 Oct): B2B has an evidence problem, not an ROI problem.
  7. 3–4 Oct. Bounds over sign-offs. Willison (3 Oct) wants default hard budget caps. Rajesh Beri (4 Oct): gate only actions that are hard to reverse and visible outside the system, and sample-audit the rest.

Verdict

Reframed. Verification stayed the bottleneck, but per-item checking moved to machines, so the scarce human work ended the month as defining what good looks like, auditing the grader and choosing which irreversible actions keep a human gate.

Counter-evidence

Anthropic's study (7 Aug; cited again 4 Oct): 1,053 paid testers caught a swapped-in dangerous command 13.6% of the time, against 89% for the classifier, and humans fell to ~5% after 50+ prompts. Add hillclimb's gains and Tunguz's taste loop, and the case is that judgment gets cheap wherever it can be encoded, with per-item human review as the weak link. Caveats: Anthropic still advises human review of high-stakes production changes, and its classifier misses 17% of real overeager actions (March). Rock Lambros (29 Sep, citing Ting Yan's August preprint) finds automated reviewers let overreach through too. Pragmatic Engineer's 24 Sep Pulse says code review will probably go away (reasoning paywalled; unconfirmed).

Reading list

  • AI Productivity Doesn't Mean What I Thought It Means (https://tomtunguz.com/ai-productivity-doesnt-mean-what-i-thought/), 2 Sep. The dataset, and the taste loop that complicates it.
  • What is happening with code reviews? (https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-happening-with-code-reviews), 8 Sep. How labs moved humans to reviewing reviews; partly paywalled.
  • Thinking in Systems, Shipping in Loops (https://tomtunguz.com/thinking-in-systems/), 24 Sep. Verification as loop design.
  • Automating eval design and hillclimbing with Claude (https://claude.dev/blog/automating-eval-design-and-hillclimbing/), 28 Sep. Where Anthropic itself keeps humans.
  • Claude's new auto eval tool (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/claude-auto-evals/), 30 Sep. Why you look at data before writing evals.
  • Auto mode is now the default in Claude Code for Pro, Max, and Team plans (https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code), 7 Aug. The strongest counter: humans caught 13.6%.

Throughline

  1. Hand-grade 20 recent AI outputs (emails, ads, briefs) yourself, then check that your automated grader agrees before you trust it.
  2. List the agent actions that are irreversible or external (sends, spend, publishing, permissions). Keep human sign-off only there, cap spend, and spot-audit the rest weekly.
  3. Attach a receipt to every AI deliverable (sources, checks run, who approved) so the board's spend question gets evidence.
