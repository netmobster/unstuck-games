DATA// The five claims

Claim 4: the wrapper is the margin

Research report, claim 4: the claim traced through September

Research agent report, 5 Oct 2026. Verbatim. Numbers and links are the agent's; CC checks every link before anything is published.

Appearances

  • 28 Aug–4 Sep. AI pays by ending re-explanation, not by generating: Cutler's recontextualization tax, Ouyang's Slack surface, Lenny's nine bots, Kramer's context layers and shared skills. Flag: Cutler's own definition is narrower (flattening detailed work for leaders) and never mentions tools or sessions.
  • 5–11 Sep. Context becomes infrastructure: Stripe's context, governance and shared skills platform.
  • 12–18 Sep. The wrapper is the margin: HarnessTax, Jev, Vercel's stack, Willison on OpenRouter, plus containers (Brex, DHH, MKT1). Counter: Gas Town, Databricks +60%. Flags: the 71% is one data point (GPT-5.6 Sol moved from Claude Code to Pi, $1.540 to $0.441 per resolved task), not an average. The five-rung ladder looks like Nate Jones's framework about DHH's Omarchy rather than DHH's own (unconfirmed).
  • 19–25 Sep. Folded into verification: Cursor's 7% harness saving, and Lambert on swarms needing verifiable problems.
  • 26 Sep–2 Oct. The harness is the product: Aakash Gupta, Pi 1.0, Alex Zhang, Thariq Shihipar. Counter: Mollick.

Background

The term was already around before September: Addy Osmani's 19 April essay argued a great harness can beat a better model. Late August made it urgent. Latent Space priced Astra at under $6 an hour, and Pragmatic Engineer found firms roughly halving their bills by routing simple work to open models. So where does the value go?

Why it matters

If the wrapper sets the bill, then harness choice, routing and deletion are levers you control without waiting for a model release. HarnessTax's headline swap (71%) beat September's list-price cuts (Opus 5.5 down 20%, GPT-6 Sol down 50%, per Willison). For marketers, the asset that lasts is a shared system that passes MKT1's vacation test, kept thin because model releases keep deleting parts of it.

The shift

  1. 22 Aug (31 Aug backfill brief, score 18). The counter came first. McAteer (Latent Space) measured harness progress by how much gets deleted. He cited a 23.8-point Harness-Bench spread for one model across harnesses, and Anthropic deleting 80% of Claude Code's system prompt.
  2. 3–9 Sep. The claim takes shape around context and skills. Alibaba.com's president Kuo Zhang (Fortune, 9 Sep): getting work done takes model, harness and context together, and the best model on his team's benchmark finished 61.7% of tasks. Arjmandi's arXiv study (8 Sep) finds no clear average effect of the harness on quality.
  3. 15–19 Sep. Numbers arrive, mostly on cost. Tunguz on Vercel (15 Sep): the bottleneck is the codified workflow, not the model. Jev claims >100x speed and >200x lower cost (16 Sep; vendor figures). HarnessTax finds one significant quality difference in 42 comparisons, and its 71% came from switching to the minimal harness (17 Sep; brief score 19). Six Jev clones ship in two days (19 Sep).
  4. 23–25 Sep. Part of Cursor's 7% cut came from deleting things: a system prompt 66% shorter because newer models need fewer guardrails, and 60% of tool-definition tokens loaded only when needed (23 Sep). An arXiv paper estimates a Jev-based router recovers 13–21% of model spend in a simulated 10,000-seat company (24 Sep). Stripe paying $7B for OpenRouter puts a price on a wrapper (Latent Space, 25 Sep).
  5. 29–30 Sep. Thariq says the core harness must get more complex and secure, but "in the limit, Claude.md goes away." OpenAI shipped a Jev competitor, the Decisions API, in about a week (Latent Space, 30 Sep). The lab is absorbing the routing layer.
  6. 1–2 Oct.
    • Aakash's guide names harness engineering the third discipline, but its free half cites no study (brief score 14).
    • Kuo Zhang recasts agents as model × harness × context (The AI Show), and logistics trade press picks up the term.
    • Mollick (score 18) argues AI learns organisation and context by itself.
    • Alex Zhang calls RLMs the bitter lesson applied to context management. He also cites about $40M at public prices (130B output tokens) for the 10,000-agent swarm Mollick describes.
    • Ramp's index (The Decoder): spend fell while usage rose about 50% since July, almost entirely because of OpenAI–Anthropic price competition.
  7. 3–5 Oct. The harness's job shifts from instructing the model to setting its limits:
    • Willison wants hard budget caps on by default (3 Oct).
    • Pi pod runs agents in sandboxes on your own server (4 Oct).
    • OpenAI's Tibo Sottiaux, on Lenny's, calls agent-workflow loops and graphs a passing phase (4 Oct, paywalled).
    • A NextLeap workshop teaches harness engineering (5 Oct).

Verdict

Reframed. The margin is real and now measured, but it's a cost margin won with thinner wrappers and hard limits, not a capability margin won with more scaffolding, and the labs are taking over the routing layer.

Counter-evidence

Mollick, The Dot and the Swarm (1 Oct): by the bitter lesson, general agents learn the organising and context work that scaffolding does. Harness builders half-agree (McAteer, Thariq, Cursor's trimming). Two September studies find harnesses barely move average quality. Ramp puts falling spend down to model price competition. The weak spot in the counter: its flagship swarm cost about $40M in tokens.

Reading list

  • The Harness Margin Opportunity (https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/), Tom Tunguz, 17 Sep 2026. The 71%, with its statistical limits in the footnotes.
  • Improved token efficiency for longer agent runs (https://cursor.com/blog/improved-token-efficiency), Cursor, 23 Sep 2026. Production savings tested with A/B tests, several of them from deleting things.
  • The Evolution of the Agent Harness (https://www.latent.space/p/attention-interface), Dan McAteer, Latent Space, 22 Aug 2026. The deletion thesis, with numbers.
  • Claude Code's Next Era — Thariq Shihipar, Anthropic (https://www.latent.space/p/thariq), Latent Space, 29 Sep 2026. What a harness builder expects to keep and to drop.
  • The Dot and the Swarm (https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-dot-and-the-swarm), Ethan Mollick, 1 Oct 2026. The counter at full strength.
  • We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/), Simon Willison, 3 Oct 2026. The case for capping spend.

Throughline

  1. Before your next model swap, keep the model the same and price one recurring workflow per completed task under two harnesses: yours and a minimal one such as Pi.
  2. Do a deletion pass on one agent. Cut the instructions, guardrails and tool definitions the current model no longer needs, load tools only when needed, and keep only what a rerun shows you need.
  3. Put the time saved into limits: a per-run and monthly spend cap that stops the agent when hit, a sandbox for anything with write access, and calls that only return a label or route moved to a decision model.
