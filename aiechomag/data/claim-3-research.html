DATA// The five claims

Claim 3: model choice stopped being the variable (with: capability got cheap)

Research report, claim 3: the claim traced through September

Research agent report, 5 Oct 2026. Verbatim. Numbers and links are the agent's; CC checks every link before anything is published.

Appearances

  • 28 Aug–4 Sep: Capability got cheap: Astra at under $6/hour, bills halved by routing simpler work to open models, and Fable 5.1's cache cut. (The same issue also called model choice "a governance decision.")
  • 5–11 Sep: Model choice is the least consequential choice in the stack: Jones's same-brief Fable/Astra build, Grok Bot ≈ OpenClaw, and Zvi on rival "most powerful" claims.
  • 12–18 Sep: "Stop asking which model to use": HarnessTax, Jev, Vercel's bill and OpenRouter variance. Databricks' +60% Astra cost appears only as a footnote.
  • 19–25 Sep: The price war was left out of the takeaways because the model "stopped being the variable." Correction: Opus 5.5's list price fell 20% ($5/$25 to $4/$20). The 40% figure is Anthropic's cost-to-run claim. Only Sol and Luna fell 50% (Willison; AINews).
  • 26 Sep–2 Oct: Price is outrunning judgment, so routing is a skill and evals prove it.

Background

Prices were falling before September. OpenAI cut Luna 80% in July, while Anthropic held Opus at $5/$25 across five releases (Tunguz, 23 Sep). Pragmatic Engineer (3 Sep) called moving simpler workloads to open models the easiest way to save about 50%.

Why it matters

Within three days, Anthropic, OpenAI and Google each priced a model at $2/$10, so the sticker price no longer separates vendors. Bills now diverge on spend per task: which model gets which job, how many tokens it burns, and the harness around it. For marketers, the budget conversation is a routing table priced per finished asset, not a vendor pick.

The shift

  • 3–5 Sep: The founding evidence was already per job. AINews found Astra cost 2.5x more per token than its predecessor but far less per task. Zvi planned to run Fable 5.1 and Astra side by side: Astra led FrontierMath Tier 4 (97.6% vs 87.8%) and Fable led on trading intuition. Latent Space's Grok Bot reviewer wanted a model picker for cheap, simple work.
  • 10 Sep: Jones's piece is titled "Which Model Gets Which Job" and concludes: "The same project can give me a reason to use both."
  • 12–17 Sep: The frontier held at $10/$50 for Astra and Fable 5.1 (Zvi). In HarnessTax, the same model fell from $1.54 to $0.44 per resolved task. Tunguz concedes a quality gap wasn't ruled out, and says cheap routing needs task-level knowledge. At Databricks, coding spend on Astra rose 60%, with gains only on complex work.
  • 22–23 Sep: Sol fell to $2/$10, Luna to $0.10/$0.50, and Opus 5.5 to $4/$20 with roughly Fable 5.1's intelligence (Willison). Open models carry most tokens at an 86% discount, and corporate frontier consumption fell from 53% to 45% (Tunguz). Yet Claire Vo's blind test split by task: Sol won writing, Opus 5.5 won long agent runs and frontend. AINews moved its pipeline to Opus 5.5 for writing quality.
  • 26 Sep: Zvi's job map: Opus 5.5 by default, Fable for expert-domain correctness, Astra for ambitious coding and science, Sol for budget work.
  • 28–30 Sep: Three labs launched models at $2/$10. Sonnet 5.5 kept its list price but costs up to 30% less per task because it uses fewer tokens. GPT-6.1 Sol ties Astra on DeepSWE and trails by about 2 points on OSWorld 2.0. Gemini 4 Argon is frontier-class, on an introductory price with restricted access. OpenAI shipped a Decisions API for routing.
  • 1 Oct: Same index score, different bill. Argon matches Astra at 53 on Artificial Analysis' index but averages 62K output tokens per task to Astra's 27K. A fifth of the token price therefore buys $1.99 against $3.26 per task (AINews). Vercel's gateway sends 60% of spend to open weights (Pragmatic Engineer). AWS and Cloudflare released decision models.
  • 2–5 Oct: Jones says habits carry across models (2 Oct), then routes demanding work to Astra and the rest to Sol to manage capacity (3 Oct). Willison calls for default hard budget caps (3 Oct). Reflection's open-weight Beam claims GLM-5.2-level scores on 3–4x less compute; its weights are pending (5 Oct).

Verdict

Reframed. The price and parity half strengthened. The evidence moved the claim from model choice not mattering to model choice mattering per job, with routing and evals as the skill. The 2 Oct roundup ended up there too.

Counter-evidence

Databricks rolled Astra out to about 3,500 engineers. Coding spend rose 60%, gains were clear only on highly complex work, and simpler coding may not have improved. Databricks responded with a dedicated Astra sub-budget (Latent Space AINews, 17 Sep). The model set both the cost and the outcome. Against the "layer" half, Mollick argues that better models absorb hand-built scaffolding ("The Dot and the Swarm," 1 Oct).

Reading list

  • Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, and a new price war (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/22/opus-and-sol-and-luna/), 22 Sep. The list-price record.
  • The Most Important Market in AI is the Middle (https://tomtunguz.com/the-most-important-market-in-ai-is-the-middle/), 23 Sep. Where tokens and spend actually go.
  • The Harness Margin Opportunity (https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/), 17 Sep. Wrapper economics, caveats included.
  • [AINews] Reality Checks on AI News (Yegge shuts down Gas Town, Databricks' +60% Astra cost) (https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-reality-checks-on-ai-news), 17 Sep. The strongest counter-case.
  • [AINews] Gemini 4 Argon: GDM's answer to Astra/Fable, with 1M output (https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-gemini-4-argon-gdms-answer), 1 Oct. Price per token against cost per task.
  • Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra: Which Model Gets Which Job (https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/fable-astra-which-job), 10 Sep. The roundups' lead source argues for routing by job.

Throughline

  1. Re-price your three most frequent AI jobs per accepted output. Run each on two $2/$10 models (Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol), logging tokens and cost.
  2. Write a routing table (job, default model, escalation trigger), and put $10/$50 frontier models behind a capped sub-budget, as Databricks did.
  3. Build a rerunnable eval from 10–20 past outputs per job, and set a hard monthly spend cap for each job.

Unconfirmed: Beam's efficiency is a vendor claim. 6.1 Sol's benchmarks come via AINews. Jones's 65-check list and Pragmatic Engineer's workload details are paywalled.
