Fact-check, 5 October, before layout

Fact-check, Monday 5 October 2026. This is a report only: nothing in drafts/ or cc-sections/ was edited.

Sources: the manus-pack (brief, rules, monthly, research, checks, roundups, daily briefs, scored archive) and every linked page, loaded with curl on 5 October. All 15 Field Notes links, the 28 Library links, the 5 roundup links and the 6 reserve links returned 200. OpenAI's research-acceleration page returned 403 on the first try and loaded on the second. Both Tunguz essays show a 5 October edit date, so I also read their Wayback captures (3 and 17 September); the wording that matters hadn't changed. Third-party wording is paraphrased below. Each source is named, so the exact text can be read there.

Unit counted: one factual statement (a sentence, a bullet, a ledger row, or a Source/From/Checked line). Opinion, advice and [placeholders] aren't counted. "Pending" means the claim describes something that hasn't happened yet, so it's UNCONFIRMED until it does.

UNCONFIRMED (12 lines)

2. The roundup corrections

16 lists six, and 17 and the edit notes carry four of them (Meta, ZCode, Cutler, OpenAI). One ruling each:

  1. Meta's 92% was backwards (28 Aug to 4 Sep): CONFIRMED. The roundup's takeaway and the 4 Sep brief call it a 92 percent premium, and the roundup's list says "The 92 percent premium". Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 page lists two tiers:

    • the contributor tier, marked as used to improve Meta's products: $0.10 input, $0.20 output
    • the standard tier, marked as not used for that: $1.25 input, $4.25 output

    That's 92% off input and 95% off output, so keeping data private costs 12.5 times as much per input token. Tunguz's essay body calls it a discount on both, and his 3 September Wayback capture says the same. 17's line can run as written.

  2. "OpenAI disclosed 3.14" (5 to 11 Sep): CONFIRMED as an error, and now settled. OpenAI's page gives 3.1 agent-workdays for every human workday as of mid-August, and Tunguz's footnote says 3.14 is his rounding. The roundup's wording follows his page's "In short" box, which his markdown stores as ai_summary: the same trap as 47-to-3. Suggested 17 line: "OpenAI disclosed 3.1, not 3.14 (5 to 11 Sep). 3.14 is Tom Tunguz's deliberate rounding, from his page's summary box; OpenAI's own page says 3.1." Delete "is unconfirmed" and "couldn't be read".

  3. Cutler didn't hand the AI the map (12 to 18 Sep): CONFIRMED, with two fixes. He does ask Notion's AI to add lines to his subway map, and he undoes it at once. He builds the map by hand. But earlier in the same post he used Notion's AI to turn the map into Notion tables, so 17's flat "didn't hand the AI the map" overstates it. Suggested: "Cutler built his map by hand; when he asked Notion's AI to extend it, he undid the result." The whole post is free, so drop "the public text of". The 14 Sep brief's claim that Notion's AI tagged his artifacts isn't in the post either.

  4. ZCode had already been fixed (19 to 25 Sep): CONFIRMED, with fixes. The brief and the roundup both use the present tense. The evidence:

    • Z.ai said it was fixed on 18 Sep.
    • ferstar's 19 Sep update found the upload code stripped from client 3.14.0 and the credential endpoint returning 404.
    • His 23 Sep update found no upload code in 3.14.3.

    Cite the 18 and 19 September dates. The 23rd falls after the 21 Sep brief, so it doesn't show that the fix came first. Keep "Z.ai says": ferstar warns that a matching version tag doesn't prove identical binaries, and he still recommends the folder lock. The correction also misses something. By 19 Sep it was known that the 313MB archive never left his machine, so the brief's line about it shipping before every prompt was already wrong when it ran.

  5. Vercel's "10 people to 1.25" (16's extra): REFUTED. SaaStr gives both about one and a quarter people and one person in the US plus about a fifth of a person, and Tunguz says 1.25. There's nothing to correct. The agent's August 2025 launch is confirmed. If anything runs, it's that the numbers predate September (section 1, item 5).

  6. Ptacek's rules (16's extra): facts CONFIRMED, no correction needed. The page has no visible byline, and its copyright line and author metadata name Thomas and Erin Ptacek. But it's written in the first person singular, and Willison, the roundup's source, credits Thomas Ptacek. The roundup isn't wrong.
