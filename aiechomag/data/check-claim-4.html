Fact-check, 5 Oct 2026. Scope: post.md lines 158–197, plus the Cutler, HarnessTax and DHH bullets (lines 244, 247 and 250). I checked roundup quotes against the saved texts in [working file] and treated curly and straight apostrophes as the same. Report only: nothing in post.md or the research notes was edited.

1. Needs a change

  1. WRONG · Background: "Late August made it urgent. Latent Space priced GPT-6 Astra… and Pragmatic Engineer found companies roughly halving their bills…" · Both pieces are dated 3 September 2026, not late August. They ran in the 28 Aug–4 Sep roundup, which is probably where "late August" came from. The figures themselves are right (see Confirmed). · Latent Space, "GPT-6 Astra: an automated AI Engineer you can hire for <$6 an hour," 3 Sep 2026, https://www.latent.space/p/astra · Pragmatic Engineer, "The Pulse: tech companies move to open AI models," 3 Sep 2026, https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-tech-companies-move-to

  2. WRONG · Why it matters: "HarnessTax's headline swap saved 71% per resolved task" · The 71% and the per-resolved-task basis are right. But the 71% is Tunguz's lead example, not HarnessTax's headline. Tunguz: "The harness swap is GPT-5.6 Sol moving from Claude Code to Pi in the same study : $1.540 to $0.441 per resolved task… That 71% reduction is measured data & is what the argument rests on." HarnessTax's own headline, as GIGAZINE reports it: "While the success rate of tasks did not vary significantly, the cost could differ by up to five times." Up to 5x means gaps of up to about 80%, so the 71% isn't even the study's largest. · Tom Tunguz, 17 Sep 2026, https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/ · GIGAZINE, 17 Sep 2026, https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260917-harnesstax-ai-coding-agents/ (HarnessTax's own page did not load; see note below)

  3. WRONG · Why it matters: "…more than any of September's list-price cuts" · One September list-price cut was bigger than 71%. Claude Fable 5.1: "Cache reads now cost 75% less, or $0.25 per million tokens." Secondary coverage dates the release 1 Sep, and the 28 Aug–4 Sep roundup covered it. The claim holds only for input/output token prices, where the biggest September cut was 50%. Willison: "GPT-5.6 Sol was $4/M input and $20/M output; GPT-6 Sol is $2/M input and $10/M output". Luna was halved and Opus 5.5 fell 20%. Anthropic puts Fable 5.1's effective saving at "around 25%" for typical workloads and "up to around 45%" for agentic ones, so the point survives if narrowed to token prices or effective cost. · Anthropic, "Introducing Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1," Sep 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1 · Simon Willison, 22 Sep 2026, https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/22/opus-and-sol-and-luna/

  4. WRONG · "Alibaba.com's president, Kuo Zhang, told Fortune that work takes the model, the harness and the context together" · This is Zhang's own bylined op-ed ("By Kuo Zhang," President of Alibaba.com), not an interview, so "wrote in Fortune." The substance is right: "Work gets done by an agent: the model that thinks, a harness that gives it tools and memory and the ability to act, and context that tells it what a good outcome looks like in a specific industry." · Fortune, 9 Sep 2026, https://fortune.com/2026/09/09/alibaba-president-kuo-zhang-ai-agents-can-talk-but-not-work/

  5. WRONG · "HarnessTax found one significant quality difference in 42 comparisons" · No difference survives correction. Tunguz, footnote 1: "Across all 42 within-model harness comparisons, a two-sided Fisher exact test finds one result at p < 0.05, where chance alone would produce about two, & none survives a Holm-Bonferroni correction… The quality gap is undemonstrated at this sample size, not proven absent." HarnessTax's own summary (via GIGAZINE) says success rate "did not vary significantly." Attribution is also shaky. The Fisher/Holm test appears only in Tunguz's footnote, which goes on to discuss limits of "the published rollouts". That reads like Tunguz reanalysing the published data, so "HarnessTax found" may misattribute it. · Tom Tunguz, 17 Sep 2026, https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/ · GIGAZINE, 17 Sep 2026, https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260917-harnesstax-ai-coding-agents/

  6. WRONG · "3 to 5 Oct… Pi shipped a way to run agents in sandboxes on your own server" · Two problems:

    • Pi didn't ship it. Pi pod is a third-party project built on Pi. The repo is under the pi-pod org with the license "Copyright © 2026 Billzo, LLC," and the HN submitter (edverma2) presents it as a tool built for their own team. HN title: "Show HN: Pi pod – Run your pi coding agent in sandboxes on your own server."
    • Wrong window. The Show HN went up on 2 Oct 2026 at 19:10 UTC (HN API timestamp 1790968238), before the 3–5 Oct window.

    Sources: Hacker News, 2 Oct 2026, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49937304 · https://github.com/pi-pod/pipod

  7. WRONG · Best case against: "Alex Zhang priced the swarm Mollick describes at about $40M in tokens" · The host, swyx, gave the figure, not Alex Zhang. swyx: "10,000 agents in 88 hours. 130 billion output tokens, which is estimated to be about 40 million dollars in public pricing." Alex Zhang replied: "Surprisingly, actually, like, less than I thought." It is the same swarm Mollick describes: both give 88 hours. · Latent Space, "Academia is for Ambition — Alex Zhang, MIT," 2 Oct 2026, https://www.latent.space/p/rlm

  8. WRONG · What I got wrong: "…and its 42 comparisons found one significant quality difference, not none" · The correction overcorrects. One comparison reached p < 0.05 uncorrected, fewer than chance would produce, and "none survives a Holm-Bonferroni correction." HarnessTax's own summary says success rates "did not vary significantly." So the 12–18 Sep roundup's "no significant quality difference" matched the sources. What it overstated was certainty: Tunguz calls the gap "undemonstrated at this sample size, not proven absent." · Tom Tunguz, 17 Sep 2026 (footnote 1), https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/ · GIGAZINE, 17 Sep 2026

  9. UNCONFIRMED (but "not DHH's" is close to settled, and the evidence leans to Nate's) · What I got wrong: "DHH's five rungs of agent access may not be DHH's… They look like Nate B Jones's framework about DHH's setup. Unconfirmed either way." · Evidence:

    • The only source for the ladder is Nate's own post. Its free preview lists "the five rungs of access an agent can ask for" among the post's own contents. The rungs themselves are behind the paywall.
    • No DHH source has a ladder. omarchy.org documents no tiered access model; its tagline is "The malleable OS for the age of agents." A write-up of DHH's Rails World 2026 keynote (Sublime Coding, 24 Sep) finds no levels of agent access.
    • DHH's own design runs the other way. His Omarchy PR is titled "Launch the default coding agent without permission prompts" (basecamp/omarchy #6729), and Omarchy's launcher "starts most coding agents with a skip-approval flag by default."
    • A derivative piece doesn't credit DHH either. Supersede Media (11 Sep) built on Nate's Omarchy video and presents a similar ladder as its own: "observation… proposed patch… scoped user-level change… Installation and administrator access." It cites no DHH source.
    • Your own roundup was split. The 12–18 Sep list entry credits the ladder to Nate's piece ("The five-rung access ladder underneath is a permission model you can apply this week"), while the takeaway says "DHH laid out five rungs."

    Verdict: almost certainly not DHH's, and very likely Nate's framework about Omarchy. Only Nate's paywalled post body would prove the second half. The bullet is accurate as written. You could firm it up to "Nate B Jones's ladder, not DHH's" with little risk.

    Sources: Nate's Newsletter, "Omarchy lets an AI agent rewrite the whole desktop. Here are 8 changes you can make on your Mac instead.," 11 Sep 2026 (paywalled; preview only), https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/omarchy-agentic-os · https://omarchy.org/ · https://github.com/basecamp/omarchy/pull/6729 · https://sublimecoding.com/blog/dhh-rails-world-2026-keynote · https://supersede.media/insights/omarchy-ai-desktop/
