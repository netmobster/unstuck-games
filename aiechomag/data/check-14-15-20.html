Check: 14 The case against, 15 The Operator, 20 The Operator's Page

Fact-check, 5 October, before layout

Checked Mon 5 Oct 2026. Scope: every tagged claim in drafts/14-the-case-against.md, drafts/15-the-operator.md and drafts/20-the-operators-page.md. None of the three carries a [NEW:] tag, so every tag points into the pack: the monthly, the five check files, one research-file header and the brief. Where research and check disagree, I followed the check.

I fetched two primary pages once each, because 14's argument leans on them: Mollick's "The Dot and the Swarm" and Anthropic's auto-mode post. Both loaded. The fetch tool returns model-extracted text, so confirm the exact wording of those quotes before any of it goes into print.

This is a report only; the drafts are unchanged. Counting unit: one source-map row is one claim, so each row covers the numbers, names, dates and quotes of one printed sentence.

1. Needs a change

  1. WRONG (minor) · 14 (line 11) · "Three of the five claims were reframed by one force. Checking moved to machines, the wrapper got thinner, and the labs began taking over routing" (source map row 2: "claims 1, 3 and 4").
    • What the cited places say: two of the three shifts are claim 4's. Intro bullet 4 reads "a cost margin won by deleting things, and the labs are taking over routing", and the §4 Verdict reads "the labs are taking over the routing layer".
    • Claim 3 points the other way. Its bullet is "The vendor matters less. Which model gets which job matters more", and its verdict gives routing to the operator: "with routing and evals as the skill".
    • So the three shifts come from two claims, 1 and 4.
    • Source: 01 intro bullets; §3 Verdict; §4 Verdict.
  2. WRONG (minor, scope) · 14 (line 11) · "The issue calls that the human step moving up a level".
    • What the draft does: "that" covers all three shifts, the labs' decision models included.
    • What the monthly does: it uses the phrase once, as the title of claim 1's step 2, "3 to 9 Sep: the human step moves up a level". That step covers the DeepMind swarm's agent audits and engineers reviewing the AI's review. The Decisions API sits under "limits over sign-offs" (§1 step 5).
    • The issue's own Pattern piece uses the phrase just as narrowly (drafts/11-the-pattern.md, line 22).
    • Source: 01 §1 steps 2 and 5.

There are no UNCONFIRMED lines. Every tagged place is in the pack, and I read each in full.

2. Other flags

Fix before print (14: untagged lines that the pack or a primary page contradicts)

  • "Nothing in his argument stops there." (about Mollick) His essay sets its own limits. One fetch of https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-dot-and-the-swarm (Ethan Mollick, 1 Oct) returned these passages:

    • "None of this means agents can do everything. AI is still too limited to substitute for large amounts of human work".
    • Agents organise "as long as humans are guiding them in the right direction".
    • On OpenAI pointing the swarm: "the division itself seems right, at least for now".
    • "the Hugging Face Incident is a reminder that self-organizing systems can head in unexpected directions".
    • He also notes that GPT-6.1 Astra was shelved because "in testing it acted without permission and misreported what it had done".

    The pack's raw research reads him the same way: "agents organise themselves and humans choose the target" (03-research/claim-1-verification.md, The shift step 6). His argument stops at human direction, which is close to the job this issue keeps for people: defining what good looks like. This sentence is the hinge of the piece's central claim.

  • "But price is the one thing September cut reliably." It has no tag, and the pack says otherwise.

    • Astra launched "2.5 times pricier per token than its predecessor" (01 §3 step 1).
    • "the frontier held at $10/$50" (§3 step 2).
    • Argon's standard price is double its introductory one (check-claim-3).
    • The cuts were all below the frontier. Sol and Luna were halved, and Willison measures that against GPT-5.6's promotional prices (check-claim-3, "Also worth knowing"). Opus 5.5 fell 20%, and Fable 5.1's cache reads fell 75% (check-claim-4).

    The cost objection is about a frontier-scale swarm, and frontier prices did not fall in September.

  • "A guardrail is a limit written in prose, and September's harness builders spent the month deleting them." No tag, and two problems.

    • Timing. Anthropic's 80% deletion was reported on 22 August. Shihipar "said that the team recently deleted 80% of Claude Code's system prompt" (McAteer, Latent Space; check-claim-4), and 01 §4 step 1 is dated "22 Aug to 9 Sep". It isn't September's work. Cursor's cut (23 Sep) is the only September deletion the piece cites.
    • What was deleted. The source says system prompt. The printed sentence, "Anthropic deleted 80% of Claude Code's.", never says so, and under this lead sentence it reads as 80% of its guardrails.
    • The monthly keeps instructions and limits apart. §4 throughline 2 deletes "instructions, guardrails and tool definitions", and throughline 3 says "Spend the savings on limits". §4 step 5 is titled "from instructions to limits", and the §4 Verdict says the margin was "won by deleting scaffolding and adding limits".

  • "The limits with teeth came from elsewhere", as applied to the six legal actions of 28 Sep to 1 Oct. check-claim-2 (Confirmed item 5): "none of them assigns liability yet: they are filings, a subpoena, a reported probe, a hearing and a bill with no text." AB 316 and the Astra cancellation have teeth; the naming doesn't yet.

Consider (14)

  • "Fewer gates won't rescue the human: at their freshest, the testers caught about one dangerous command in six."

    • The study measured detection inside a run of permission prompts. It never tested a sparse gate on irreversible actions.
    • The post doesn't define "early in a session" (fetch of https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code, 7 Aug).
    • The same post has evidence pointing the other way, and the piece doesn't face it: "when Claude presents a plan for approval, users reject 39% of them. But for individual permissions requests, the rejection rate is only 3%." Draft 08 cites this with a [NEW] tag, and the passage was on the page when I fetched it.

  • "The pattern's best evidence is that four of five throughlines end at a hard spend cap." Neither the monthly nor the issue calls it that.

    • The monthly's pattern is the five verdicts moving from "a statement about what was scarce" to "a limit someone has to set".
    • It brings in the cap as "One action turned up in four of the five throughlines" and discounts it in the same paragraph.
    • The issue's Pattern piece says "The evidence for it counts once" (11, line 91).

    The section argues against a leg the issue has already discounted.

  • "Astra searches a median of five sources [S]. The operator doesn't set where a brand gets cited. The model does." This joins searched and cited back together, which is the brief's trap 6. check-claim-5 item 1 flagged the same pairing in the monthly's throughline. A search count doesn't show who gets cited.

  • "The limits that survive end up in the vendor's harness, not the operator's." No tag. It's an inference from Shihipar's two remarks; the source says only that the core harness "has to be like quite complex and very secure".

  • "expects the core harness to get more complex and more secure" · The source says "has to be like quite complex and very secure" (check-claim-4), and the monthly says "has to get more complex". "Expects" turns a necessity into a forecast. Minor.

  • "The widest saving of the month wasn't a limit either." No tag. Nothing in the pack ranks the month's savings.

  • AWS and Cloudflare offered as "the labs". They are cloud platforms. The monthly's "labs are taking over routing" rests on OpenAI's Decisions API (§4 step 4). Minor.

  • "OpenAI shipped its Decisions API" (14) against "OpenAI previewed its Decisions API on 29 September" (15). Both are supportable. §4 step 4 and check-claim-4 say shipped. check-claim-1 says it "launched as a limited preview, so 'shipped' is fair but loose". Pick one for the issue.

  • Source map row 32 points to check-claim-2 "Confirmed items 22 to 27". Those cover Florida, the FTC, the hearing, the attorney general and the bill, but not the non-profit. LASST's source is check-claim-2 Needs-a-change item 9 and "The two lookups" (TNW, 30 Sep).

  • The three derived figures (rows 17, 20 and 25) have no inline tag. They appear in the source map only.

15 and 20

  • 15 · Hawley–Murphy: "Its text isn't out yet." This is undated. 20 dates the same fact ("as of 5 October"), and 15 could do the same.
  • 15 · "success rates did not vary significantly". This is HarnessTax's own wording (via GIGAZINE), so the line is CONFIRMED.

  • 15 · "Fewer prompts means less fatigue at the ones that matter." No tag. It follows from the post's "As sessions got longer, humans did worse", but nobody measured a sparse gate. 14 reads the same 17% the opposite way, and the 39% plan-rejection figure supports 15 here.

  • Mollick. The page reads "It turns out that organizing work is just one more thing AI can learn to do." 14 and check-claim-4 capitalise "Organizing"; strict style would be "[O]rganizing".
  • Shihipar. "in the limit, Claude.md goes away" is cut from "I do think in the limit, Claude.md goes away, and maybe not even, like, that far." The cut doesn't distort it.

Arithmetic

The three derived figures in 14 hold:

  • "about one in six" from about 17%: 1/6 = 16.7%.
  • "more than eight in ten" missed from 13.6% caught: 910 of 1,053 = 86.4%. "Nearly nine in ten" would be closer.
  • "about three-fifths of the cost per task": $1.99 / $3.26 = 0.610.

These hold too:

  • "a fifth": $2/$10 against $10/$50.
  • $3.98 = 2 × $1.99.
  • 11% let through = 116 of 1,053, the complement of the post's 89% (937 of 1,053). The post itself states the 89%, not the 11%.
  • "two days before the research agents filed": the post is from 3 Oct, and all five reports are headed 5 Oct.
  • Fifteen actions = 5 × 3.
  • Four of five throughlines carry a cap: §1 throughline 2, §2 throughline 2, §3 throughline 3, §4 throughline 3; §5 has none.
  • Five AI tools.

The steelman's inference

The writer calls "the model eats the limits too" an inference. For the most part, the text presents it as a finding.

  • It's stated as fact four times: the title; the standfirst ("the limits go next"); the opening paragraph ("The evidence supports a harder one. The model eats the limits too"); and the closing question ("which of these limits won't the model eat?").
  • It's marked once: "He was writing about the wrapper. Nothing in his argument stops there." The first sentence concedes Mollick's scope. The second hands the step back to him, and his essay says the opposite.
  • The untagged definition "A guardrail is a limit written in prose" carries the claim. Everything under it is about deleted prompt instructions: Cursor's 66%, Anthropic's 80% and "Claude.md goes away". The monthly treats those as the opposite of limits: instructions out, limits in. Nothing in the pack shows a spend cap or a sandbox made unnecessary by a better model. The one gate a model took over, auto mode's classifier, is still a gate.
  • The later sections argue something else.
    • "The machine is the better gate" moves the gatekeeper; it doesn't remove the gate.
    • The conclusion ("labs, platforms and lawmakers set the limits above them") has the limits surviving, set by others.
    • Those claims are better sourced than the headline one. On marketing, though, the issue's Pattern table already concedes the point: "Mostly set by others" (11, line 103).
  • To read as an inference, it needs three changes:
    • one sentence that owns the step from wrapper to limits as the piece's own reading;
    • the guardrail-equals-limit line marked as a reading, not a fact;
    • a fixed price line, since the reply to the cost objection rests on it.
