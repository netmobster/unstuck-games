Audit C: the machinery

Framing audit, 5 to 6 October

The AI Echo, Edition 1. Red Team v3, point 3: "Don't explain the apparatus. Demonstrate it." Auditor C (CC), overnight 5 to 6 October 2026. Report only: no draft, check, print, design or audit file was changed. Separate editors apply what's here.

Read against. The red team's brief; print/edition-1-full.md; the annotated files it is built from (drafts/, cc-sections/), with their source maps and edit logs; the five check reports in checks/ and their tallies; edit-rulings.md; edit-notes.md; claude-design-brief.md; Claude Design's README.md, identity.html and the p02 and p12 page templates; for-claude-design/round-2/round-2-notes.md. Audits A, B1, B2 and D were already in audit/; where we overlap, the cross-reference is given and any disagreement is named (end of file). No fact was re-checked against the web.

Where to edit. Line numbers are the annotated files' (drafts/ or cc-sections/). "Print L" is the line in print/edition-1-full.md. Section numbers are the file numbers, as in the brief. The printed contents number them three lower: 04 prints as 01, 07 The Receipts as 04, 08 as 05, 11 The Pattern as 08, 17 Corrections as 14, 18 Prove us wrong as 15.

The test. For every paragraph or line about the process: does it help the reader understand the evidence, or does it only tell them the process is sophisticated? KEEP · COMPRESS (text given) · CONVERT to a stamp or ledger line (line given) · CUT. A KEEP carries a priority only when the line needs a fix to stay.

Summary

65 verdicts on process paragraphs and lines in the listed sections, 9 of them the list for Jay's letter.

KEEP COMPRESS CONVERT CUT
21 12 22 10

By priority: MUST 7 · SHOULD 26 · COULD 15 · no change 17. Part 2 adds six design items (all SHOULD). Part 3 has three notes outside the listed sections.

What passes. Where the machinery is already a demonstration, it stays: the correction entries, "One argument, found four times", the feature openers that set September's claim against October's evidence, The Month's "the verdicts are the research's readings", and Corrections' note that the live monthly is corrected in the magazine, not quietly edited.

What fails. Narration: the pipeline told in prose; five How we know boxes of 160 to 250 words each, 1,035 in all; the Manus story told three times; and Prove us wrong restating its five boxes at nearly three times its 200-word budget. And four statements about the process that aren't true of the printed issue.

The three that matter most.

  1. Make the machinery true before showing it (C-34, C-20, C-19, C-45, C-13: all MUST). Corrections says the 12 unconfirmed lines are "fixed, reworded or cut": 9 still print. The Receipts says its one unsettled line was "reworded to what could be shown": Corrections says it ran firmer than proven. The Receipts' claim-scoring step has no record behind it. Two How we know boxes point readers at NEW tags that the print strips. Rule I then promises what the open lines contradict.
  2. Stamp every section from its check tally, and make the ledger add up (D-01, C-24). The five reports' row counts sum to 647 lines, not the ledger's 642: five rows one checker counted as overstated are missing. Each page gets a steel stamp such as CHECKED // 57 LINES · 3 WRONG / ALL CORRECTED // 2026.10.05, and any line still open is named on it.
  3. Shrink the five How we know boxes to this piece's stamp, the research check as one line, a NOT CHECKED line naming the reading, and two sentences of real uncertainty (C-46, C-50, C-53, C-54, C-55): about 500 words for all five, from 1,035.

Taken together, the recommendations take roughly 1,000 words of prose out of an issue that runs about 2,000 over its flatplan, and put stamps and tables in their place.

D-01 · SHOULD · A stamp for every section

What a stamp says. Two lines, three when something is still open:

CHECKED // {lines} LINES · {wrong} WRONG
{CORRECTED | BOTH CORRECTED | ALL CORRECTED} // 2026.10.05
{n} OPEN // {what each one waits on}

When nothing was wrong, the date joins the first line: CHECKED // 54 LINES · 0 WRONG // 2026.10.05.

  • Lines is the check report's own row count for the section, so a reader can match the stamp to the report (D-04). A line is one sentence, bullet or table row with something checkable in it (the key in C-24).
  • Wrong is the report's WRONG count. Overstated lines get no term on the stamp. Only check-01-06-11 counted them apart; the other four checks flagged the same kind of line under "Other flags" and counted it confirmed (08 alone had six). An OVERSTATED figure on the page would make The Month and The Pattern look worse than 08 and 09 for following a stricter rule. The ledger carries the five (C-24).
  • Corrected is supported: I compared all 25 WRONG items in the five reports against the print copy, and every fix is in it. For the CC sections, that comparison is the only record (D-03).
  • The date as round 2 set it, 2026.10.05: "10.05" reads as 10 May outside North America.
  • No "CC". The design's receipt stamp ends "CC · 10.05". It is house shorthand the reader hasn't been told; the key and the colophon say who checks.

What it says when lines are still unconfirmed. A line the checkers couldn't confirm, and the edit didn't settle, stays on its page's stamp with what it waits on, in a few words: 3 OPEN // HIS READ · HIS REPLY · THE AI LETTERS. At press each open line is settled (and drops off the stamp), cut, or stays named. Nothing open prints unnamed. Lines about the editor's own acts (his read, his yes, his reply) are settled by the act, recorded with its date in the edit log. The three unconfirmed lines settled in the edit (Rule II reworded; "uniqueness" confirmed from the script; Field Notes 4B's redirect line cut) don't appear on stamps; the ledger counts them.

How it looks. The receipt stamp's chamfered mono block (identity.html, Labels and stamps), in steel: 2px steel border, steel text, page ground, no fill. The 1B brackets-check mark at the left: round 2's notes say it "doubles as the 'checked' stamp". D-05 · SHOULD · Evidence blocks for the few claims that carry a piece

Format. Four rows, set beside the paragraph that makes the claim:

SOURCE  // where it comes from, and how close to the primary it is
CHECK   // who checked it, against what, and what they found
STATUS  // one entry from the list below
OPEN    // what is still uncertain, or what is ours

Labels in Share Tech Mono 12px, faint; values in Source Sans 17px (the design's "Small" type for boxes). Steel corner brackets, no fill, no shadow: shadows are reserved for How we know, field cards, chart wells and the routing table. One column wide, never beside a pull quote. Where a feature carries a block, its How we know box doesn't repeat the block's OPEN line.

STATUS, one of: CONFIRMED · CORRECTED (a check found it wrong, and it's fixed) · CONFIRMED AS REPORTED (it matches a secondary source; the primary wasn't read) · OPEN (it waits on something) · OUR READING (an inference from checked facts) · OUR BET (what comes next). The last two are audit B2's labels (Part 4), so the blocks and the page labels are one system.
