DATA// The five claims

Fact-check: "## 5. Marketing moved from making with AI to being found by it"

Check, claim 5: the monthly's section read line by line against its sources

Checked 5 Oct 2026, covering section 5 of post.md plus four "What I got wrong" bullets ("Ben Thompson", "Two IAB numbers", "ChatGPT ads are working", "Three sources were thinner"). Report only; post.md and the research notes are unchanged.

1. Needs a change

  1. WRONG · "Astra cites a median of 5 sources (Sol 9, Opus 11, Fable 15)" (How it shifted, step 1) · The numbers are right but the verb isn't. The article ties the medians to searching, not citing: "Anthropic seems to be biasing their models to searching more sources (Sol median of 9 sources, vs Astra median of 5, vs Opus median of 11 sources, vs Fable of 15)." It doesn't define the count anywhere else. "Searches" or "draws on a median of 5 sources" would match. The throughline's "Astra keeps about five sources per answer" is neutral, but it sits next to "log who gets cited", which carries the same assumption. · Latent Space, 7 Sep 2026, https://www.latent.space/p/aeo

  2. WRONG · "48% of buyers now measure brand visibility inside AI tools" (step 2) · This isn't current practice. The 48% is the top answer from buyers who are changing or expect to change their measurement within 12 months: "86% of buyers already changing, or expecting to change, how they measure media performance because of conversational AI tools and AI agents in the next 12 months. The most common response, selected by 48% of buyers, is measuring brand visibility and citations directly within AI tools." · IAB, 10 Sep 2026, https://www.iab.com/news/iab-raises-2026-u-s-ad-spend-forecast/

  3. WRONG · "Erase.com found only 37.9% of the URLs cited in Google's AI answers rank in the top 10" (step 4) · This is Ahrefs' figure, which Erase.com quoted. It comes from March, not 28–30 Sep: "In March, Ahrefs studied 4 million AI Overview citations across 863,000 keyword searches. Only 37.9% of the cited URLs ranked in the top 10 for that search." Erase.com's own data is the 714 suppression campaigns. · MarTech (byline Erase.com), 30 Sep 2026, https://martech.org/what-714-suppression-campaigns-show-about-pushing-down-negative-results

  4. WRONG · "Datos found … AI mentions lifting visits to the brand's own site 1.5 to 2.5 times within a week" (step 5) · This is a Profound study, not Datos. MarTech links "[this study on the AI mention effect]" (tryprofound.com/blog/the-ai-mention-effect): "After an AI assistant mentions a brand, that user visits the brand's website at 1.5 to 2.5 times their forecasted baseline rate over the following seven days." Datos is credited only with the same-rate finding. (The 2.5% referral-tag figure under "Why it matters" is from the same Profound study.) · MarTech, 1 Oct 2026, https://martech.org/7-ai-search-myths-busted-by-data/

  5. WRONG (minor) · "Forrester questioned whether consumers want autonomous buying at all" (step 5) · "At all" goes further than Forrester does: "It's still unclear whether a critical mass of consumers actually want autonomous purchasing." The authors expect take-up in "specialized use cases". "Whether a critical mass of consumers want it" would match. · Forrester (Sucharita Kodali, Emily Pfeiffer), 2 Oct 2026, https://www.forrester.com/blogs/meta-amazon-and-the-real-question-about-agentic-commerce/

  6. WRONG (quote attribution) · 'Datos found the two "growing at about the exact same rate quarter over quarter,"' (The best case against) · The finding is right, but the quoted words are MarTech's summary (Rob Tindula), not a quote from Datos. MarTech's text, with no quote marks of its own: "The State of Search Q2 2026 report found that the two are growing at about the exact same rate quarter over quarter." The Datos/SparkToro report page doesn't use the phrase. The full report is a download I couldn't open; if it uses these exact words, this becomes CONFIRMED. Either credit the quote to MarTech or drop the quote marks. · MarTech, 1 Oct 2026, https://martech.org/7-ai-search-myths-busted-by-data/ ; https://datos.live/report/state-of-search-q2-2026/

  7. WRONG · '"ChatGPT ads are working" is unconfirmed … the only public numbers show costs per click from under $3 to $13, with no benchmarks' (What I got wrong) · The CPC range and "no benchmarks" are right: "Some advertisers report costs per click of less than $3, but others are faced with $10 or up to $13" and "there are no performance benchmarks across advertisers, industries or campaign types." But "the only public numbers" is wrong. In May, Criteo published "AI-referred conversion rates approaching 2x those of traditional search" and "CTRs approximately 3x those of comparable formats". These are vendor-reported (PPC Land: "Independent, third-party verification of conversion performance at scale remains unavailable"). This same section also cites OpenAI's public $1B run rate (31 Aug). "The only independent numbers" or "the public numbers are cost-only or vendor-reported" would hold. · MediaPost, "ChatGPT Ad Results Are Not Yet Clear" (page dated 8 Sep 2026; search listing shows 09/09/2026), https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417746/chatgpt-ad-results-are-not-yet-clear.html ; PPC Land, 6 May 2026, https://ppc.land/over-1-000-brands-now-live-on-chatgpt-ads-via-criteo-as-ai-conversions-near-2x/

  8. WRONG (minor) · "MKT1's 1,100 job posts" (What I got wrong) · The source says 1,100-plus. Title: "I analyzed 1,100+ job posts with Claude Code. Here's what B2B marketers get paid". Body: "1,100+ marketing job posts, 794 from private B2B startups". The pay-and-equity point is confirmed below. · MKT1 (Emily Kramer), 1 Oct 2026, https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/marketing-salaries-2026

No UNCONFIRMED lines. Every source loaded. MarTech returned a 429 once and loaded on retry.
