Check: claim 3, "Model choice stopped being the variable"

Check, claim 3: the monthly's section read line by line against its sources

Checked Mon 5 Oct 2026. Scope: post.md from "## 3." to "## 4.", plus the "Opus 5.5's price fell 20%" bullet under "What I got wrong". Roundup quotes were checked against the saved texts in [working file]. Third-party wording below is paraphrased except for one short quote; each URL has the exact text. Prices are per million input/output tokens.

1. Needs a change

  • WRONG · Why it matters: "Within three days at the end of September, Anthropic, OpenAI and Google each had a model at $2/$10" · The three launches did land on three consecutive days: Sonnet 5.5 on 28 Sep, GPT-6.1 Sol on 29 Sep at DevDay, and Gemini 4 Argon on 30 Sep. But "had" is wrong for two of the three labs, and Google's price comes with conditions.
    • Anthropic was already at $2/$10. Its Sonnet 5.5 page says the new model is priced the same as Sonnet 5, at $2/$10. Anthropic's pricing page says Sonnet 5 launched at $2/$10 as an introductory price through 31 Aug 2026, that this is now the standard price, and that the rise to $3/$15 planned for 1 Sep won't happen.
    • OpenAI had GPT-6 Sol at $2/$10 from 22 Sep (Willison's price table). GPT-6.1 Sol launched on 29 Sep at the same $2/$10 (Zvi, 1 Oct).
    • Google: Argon's standard price is $4/$20. A 50% introductory discount brings it to $2/$10, with no end date announced. Access starts with government users and trusted cyber defenders in Google's Fairwind Program, and developers, enterprises and consumers come later. Zvi (1 Oct) says nobody else can use it yet.
    • Correct version: between 28 and 30 Sep all three labs launched or announced a model at $2/$10. Anthropic (Sonnet 5) and OpenAI (GPT-6 Sol, since 22 Sep) were already at that price. Google's $2/$10 is an introductory half-price on $4/$20, for trusted testers only.
    • Knock-on: item 4's "a fifth of the token price bought $1.99 a task against $3.26" holds only at Argon's introductory price. AINews puts it at $3.98 a task at standard pricing, which is more than Astra's $3.26.
    • Sources: Anthropic, 28 Sep, https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5 · Claude pricing docs, footnote 3, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing · Simon Willison, 22 Sep, https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/22/opus-and-sol-and-luna/ · Willison's DevDay live blog, 29 Sep, https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/openai-devday-2026-live-blog/ · Zvi Mowshowitz, AI #188, 1 Oct, https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/10/01/ai-188-gemini-dot-argon/ · AINews (Latent Space), 1 Oct, https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-gemini-4-argon-gdms-answer (paid; the preview carries all of this)
  • WRONG · Item 3: "Zvi's routing: … Sol for budget work" · Zvi's list doesn't mention Sol. His fourth line gives cheaper work to Luna and other cheap models, for jobs that don't need the extra firepower or need an open model. The rest of the paraphrase is fine: Opus 5.5 as the default, Fable 5.1 for expert-domain correctness, Astra for ambitious coding and some science work. Correct version: "Luna and other cheap models for budget work". · Zvi Mowshowitz, "Claude Opus 5.5 Should Raise Your Ambitions", 26 Sep, https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/26/claude-opus-5-5-should-raise-your-ambitions/
  • WRONG · Where it appeared, 19 to 25 Sep: "the price war, which I left out of the takeaways" · The price war is in that roundup's first takeaway ("Access replaced capability as the constraint"): "And in the same week every frontier lab cut prices 40–50%, with Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna all shipping." The roundup contradicts itself. Its list entry says the price war "still did not make the takeaways", and its pattern says "none of it reached the takeaways." Correct version: it made the takeaways as one supporting line, not as a takeaway of its own. That line is also where the 40–50% error corrected under "What I got wrong" was printed. · saved -sept-484.txt lines 16, 66 and 126, https://echofiles.substack.com/p/this-week-in-ai-operator-space-sept-484
  • WRONG (minor) · The best case against: "simpler coding may not have improved at all" · AINews says Astra "may not materially improve medium/low-complexity coding". Later it says those tasks saw little improvement. Neither says no improvement. Correct version: "simpler coding may not have improved much". · AINews (Latent Space), 17 Sep, https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-reality-checks-on-ai-news
  • WRONG (overstated) · Item 1: "AINews found GPT-6 Astra cost 2.5 times more per token than its predecessor but far less per task" · Only the subtitle says this: 2.5x pricier per token but much cheaper per task. The body's own analysis is mixed. Astra was cheaper per task in some workflows and materially more expensive in others. The body also reports Artificial Analysis finding Astra 75% more expensive per task than GPT-5.6 Sol at max effort. Correct version: "AINews found Astra 2.5 times pricier per token, cheaper per task in some workflows and dearer in others." The mixed finding fits the per-job point better than the headline does. · AINews (Latent Space), 4 Sep, https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-gpt-6-astra-openais-biggest (paid; preview)
  • WRONG (minor) · Item 3: "Claire Vo's blind test split by task: Sol won writing, Opus 5.5 won long agent runs and front-end work" · Her public episode summary hedges. It says Opus 5.5 may be the strongest overall, especially for long-running agents and B2B front-end work, and that Sol still wins her over on clear writing, readable PRDs and price. Astra, the third model in the test, was her favourite. The post drops the hedge, the "B2B" and Astra. I couldn't check the detailed scores, because the transcript loads dynamically. Correct version: "Sol won her over on clear writing; Opus 5.5 looked strongest for long-running agents and B2B front-end work". · Claire Vo, How I AI (Lenny's Newsletter), 22 Sep, https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/opus-55-vs-gpt-6-sol-which-model
  • WRONG (minor) · Item 1: "Zvi planned to run Fable 5.1 and Astra side by side, because each led on different work" · The plan is right, but the reason isn't Zvi's. He says he compared the two on one hard question and got two different, very good answers that complemented each other, so he planned to ask both models every non-trivial query for a while. Elsewhere, the same post reports Astra ahead on FrontierMath Tier 4 (97.6% vs 87.8%). It also quotes Jane Street crediting Fable 5.1 with state-of-the-art trading intuition. Those findings support "each led on different work" as a separate statement, but not as his reason. Correct version: "because their answers complemented each other". · Zvi Mowshowitz, 5 Sep, https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/05/claude-mythos-5-1-and-fable-5-1-capabilities/
  • WRONG (trivial) · Item 2: "fell from $1.54 to $0.44 per resolved task" · Tunguz's footnote gives $1.540 and $0.441 (GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Code vs Pi), so $0.44 is a rounding the source didn't do. The 71% is unaffected. · Tomasz Tunguz, 17 Sep, https://tomtunguz.com/the-harness-margin-opportunity/

Also worth knowing (not verdicts):

  • Luna's "50%" is the sources' own rounding. Willison and Altman both call GPT-6 Sol and Luna half the price of GPT-5.6. Willison's table shows Luna's input price halving ($0.20 to $0.10) and its output price falling 58% ($1.20 to $0.50). He also notes that the 50% is measured against GPT-5.6's promotional prices.
  • "Only GPT-6 Sol and Luna fell 50%" holds for input/output prices. Opus 5.5's cache-read price fell 60%, from $0.50 to $0.20 (Anthropic).
  • "Shipped" vs "previewing" for the Decisions API. Willison's live blog says OpenAI was previewing it. Latent Space's DevDay episode calls it shipped and describes it as a tool to classify content, route requests or pick an agent's next action. Either word is supportable.
  • Tunguz's 86% has a narrower scope in his essay. His "In short" box says open models carry most token volume at an 86% discount. The essay body limits this to the gateways that publish data.
  • The AWS and Cloudflare items rest on press coverage. Both are dated 1 Oct: SiliconANGLE for AWS Strands Decider 2B and Crypto Briefing for Cloudflare's Clef and Clef-flash. No vendor page was fetched.
  • "Concluded" for the Jones quote reads fine as "his conclusion". The sentence closes the free opening of the piece, and the rest is paywalled.
