No fact was re-checked, and nothing was loaded from the web. B1's audit landed in this folder while I worked. I use its levels, its prose marker and its draft-tag fix, so the two audits make one system.

Levels. As B1:

  • RECEIPT: someone said, filed, published or measured it, or a checker confirmed it.
  • READING: the magazine's interpretation of checked facts.
  • HYPOTHESIS, or bet: what comes next, or what it means.
  • Advice isn't graded, unless it states a causal claim as settled.

One working test. A sentence whose only tag is the monthly's #the-pattern or #intro cites the editor's own earlier reading. No check covered either section (check-01-06-11, Tags), so the sentence is a reading, whatever its tag says.

Where to edit. Line numbers are the drafts'. They match print/, because make-print.mjs strips tags inside the line. Cover 1c and the spread's graphic are Claude Design's files: those fixes go to round 2 as notes.

Part 4. How the levels show in print

For Claude Design, in the ECHO-JAY system. 196 words.

Receipts already have SOURCE // under every chart. Add two labels in that style (Share Tech Mono, uppercase, 13px, steel text): OUR READING // over a paragraph, pull quote or chart mark that reads across checked facts; OUR BET // over a line on what comes next or what it means. Unlabelled copy is receipt by default. Like the box titles, labels may say "our"; feature prose stays third person.

A labelled paragraph gets a 2px dashed steel rule down its left edge. Dashed is ours, solid is sourced, in text and graphics, as Being found's stretch already is. No new colour, no radii. Gold stays the one attention mark; a gold reading carries its tag.

Whole paragraphs get the label; a lone reading sentence among receipts gets words ("on the magazine's reading"). Editors mark [READING] and [BET] in drafts, like [PULL]. Labels are words, so they survive into the Substack email.

Example, The Pattern:

OUR READING // OF CHECKED FACTS · NOT ITSELF CHECKED

Part 5. A new rule for The Rules

For cc-sections/05-the-rules-DRAFT.md, in its format:

V. A reading says it's a reading. Where we go past what our sources say, to what the facts add up to or where they lead, the page says so: our reading, or our bet. Nothing we inferred is printed as if a source had said it. The test: can you tell, from the page alone, where the evidence ends and our judgment begins?

  • Placement. After IV, "A steelman says it's a steelman", which it matches. The current V to VIII become VI to IX. Claude Design's brief specifies "Eight numbered rules", so the page spec becomes nine.
  • The test is the red team's line about showing "where the evidence ends", turned into a question a reader can check.
  • Rule I, COULD. Its heading, "Every claim has a source", is wider than its body (numbers, quotes, dates and attributions). A reading is a claim with no source of its own. With V in place, I could read "Every fact has a source."
