1. Needs a change

1. WRONG · 09, under "Four days, six names", 30 September (source-map row 36)

  • As written: "The FTC reportedly opened a probe into OpenAI and Anthropic, according to the New York Times"
  • What the sources say: the line matches item 23 of check-claim-2 word for word. But reports from the same day contradict three parts of it:
    • The hedge. The agency confirmed the probe on 30 Sep. CNBC reports that "an agency spokesperson confirmed to CNBC". TNW says the spokesperson confirmed it to both CNBC and CBS News.
    • The date. TNW reports two things. The spokesperson told CBS News the probe first opened in the summer. A senior FTC official told the New York Post it was opened before the Hugging Face incident. So 30 September is when the probe became public, not when it opened.
    • The credit. CNBC and TNW both say the New York Post reported the probe first. The New York Times credit rests on SiliconANGLE alone. SiliconANGLE describes the same details that TNW credits to the Post: civil investigative demands, and METR.
  • Suggested fix: "The FTC's probe of OpenAI and Anthropic became public, first reported by the New York Post. An FTC spokesperson confirmed it and said it had opened in the summer." Tag it NEW with the CNBC and TNW URLs.
  • Knock-on changes:
    • Line 7 and row 5: "a reported probe" becomes "a probe".
    • Row 66: update the note.
    • The monthly and item 23 of check-claim-2 use the same "reportedly" wording, so the Corrections department may want this too.
  • Sources:
    • CNBC, 30 Sep 2026
    • The Next Web, 30 Sep 2026
    • SiliconANGLE, 30 Sep 2026
    • The search results agree, among them the ABC News and Axios headlines from 30 Sep. I did not load those two pages.

2. WRONG · 09, How we know box (row 66)

  • As written: "the FTC probe is reported, not confirmed by the agency [S: 04-checks/check-claim-2.md]"
  • What the sources say:
    • The check says only "reportedly". It says nothing about whether the agency confirmed.
    • The agency did confirm, on 30 Sep: to CNBC, and to CBS News according to TNW.
  • Fix: cut the sentence, or replace it with what is still open. CNBC says the spokesperson declined to name the other companies under investigation.
  • Sources: as in item 1.

UNCONFIRMED: none. Every NEW and [S: url] page loaded on the first try.

2. Other flags

Claims stronger than their source

  1. 09, line 31 (row 20): "An incident involving your account involves a third party, which puts it on the track with no deadline."
    • The draft states this as fact, but it is the checker's inference (check-claim-2, WRONG 6).
    • The piece's own Prove us wrong box calls it "our reading".
    • TNW's text says only that complex cases involving third parties or security concerns can take longer.
    • Add "on our reading" to the body.
    • "No deadline" at line 29 and line 71 (rows 18 and 63) would match the source better as "no stated deadline". The monthly and the brief both say "no deadline", so this tightens the wording rather than correcting an error.
  2. 09, standfirst and line 9: "one of them is whoever runs the agent."
    • Of the six actions, only the bill reaches operators other than the labs, and the bill has no text.
    • The other five actions all target OpenAI, which ran its own agents.
    • Beri's 2 Oct piece makes the same point: every incident so far involved the developer's own agents in its own research runs, and the bill would reach further.
    • Consider: "and, in a bill with no text yet, one of them is whoever runs the agent."
  3. 09, line 7 (row 4): "each started naming who should answer", within the four days.
    • Florida's request is part of a suit filed on 1 June, which the draft itself says at line 41.
    • The URL of the attorney general's subpoena release calls it part of an ongoing investigation.
    • The FTC says its probe opened in the summer.
    • What fell inside the window were filings and disclosures. "Started" is loose; "went public" or "acted" is safer.
  4. 09, line 21: "September opened with the main window into agents closing." The source reports a substantial decrease in chain-of-thought monitorability. "Narrowing" matches the source; "closing" overstates it.
  5. 09, line 59: "The alternatives to liability grew too", which leads into AIUC. AIUC underwrites agents (its Latent Space episode is titled "Backing Agents you can Sue"). Insurance assumes liability rather than replacing it. This is a framing point only.
  6. 09, line 47 (row 42): "no bill number". The check, Beri and TechTimes all say "no confirmed bill number".
  7. 08, line 5 (row 1): "In August, Anthropic made auto mode the default in Claude Code."
    • The change covered new sessions on Pro, Max and Team plans from 14 August. Enterprise and the API stayed opt-in.
    • Row 1 of the source map says this; the body does not.
  8. 08, line 5 (row 3): "Anthropic's reason was in its own numbers: users approved 97%".
    • The check calls the 97% part of the reason.
    • The post also cites longer autonomous runs and about 25% more PRs.
    • "Part of Anthropic's reason" fixes it.
  9. 08, line 67 (row 17): "At Anthropic a person still presses merge on low-risk changes". The check says "for now"; put it back.
  10. 08, line 79 (row 47): "a page's AI-written summary box".
    • The check says the site's markdown labels the box ai_summary, so it was "probably machine-written".
    • The monthly says the site's markup labels it an AI summary.
    • I confirmed the field is ai_summary in the raw markdown.
    • "A summary box the site labels as AI-written" stays within the record.
  11. 08, line 61: "the record is where most organisations are thin". This generalises from two surveys: 300 US engineering leaders (Qodo) and chief marketing officers (Comviva). Mild.
  12. 08, line 43 (row 35): "found two test cases that never improved". The post gives two examples, not a count. "Found test cases that never improved, two of them…" is exact.

Quotes

Every quote in both drafts matches its source word for word. That includes the four [S: url] quotes (two from Hamel, one from claude.dev, one from Beri), Hawley's line (only the opening capital differs, as row 2 notes) and Trump's "morally binding" from CBS. Notes:

  1. 09, line 43: in the CNBC statement as TNW reports it, OpenAI first calls Hugging Face a serious incident it has acted on, and then calls the suit completely without merit. The fragment is accurate, but including the concession would be fairer to OpenAI.
  2. 09, lines 67 and 69, and 08, line 49: three passages are near-verbatim from Beri but carry no quote marks: the per-agent scope statement, the definition of the action log, and the "undo by calling a customer, bank, regulator or counterparty" test. All three are attributed. Either quote them or rephrase them.
  3. 09, line 57: "scope and authorization" is an accurate fragment. Per the check, Jain's full criterion also covered how the model communicates back to the user.
  4. 08, line 45: Hamel tested the tool together with Isaac Flath, so it asked them both. "Asked Hamel" is fine as a paraphrase.

Sourcing and context

  1. 09, How we know: "OpenAI's own incident page returned an error to the checker" sits right after the six-day and 12-day figures.
    • The 403 error in check-claim-2 (WRONG 10b) came from the page about the 100-plus notifications, not from the misalignment-report framework.

  2. 08, line 23 (row 18): "Elsewhere, the most common setup has engineers reviewing the AI's review… One example… is Anthropic's Bun project."
    • "Elsewhere" means outside the labs, but the example given is Anthropic's.
    • The check says the article describes this as the most common approach across companies in general.

  3. 09, line 41: according to WFSU, the attorney general pointed to the Australia and Hugging Face break-ins in a video on X explaining the move. WFSU does not say they are in the filing. The draft's sentence doesn't claim they are, so it holds; just don't tighten it in that direction.
