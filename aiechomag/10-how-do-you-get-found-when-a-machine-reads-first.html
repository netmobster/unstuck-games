How do you get found when a machine reads first?

In September AI discovery got money, rules and plumbing while, in the one study that counted, most of the visits it sent went untagged, and the month's data suggests it is growing on top of search rather than in place of it.

Feature // 7 min read

The ECHO Files' weekly roundups restated this claim almost every week. The first told readers to plan around Google staying as powerful as it was. The third said marketing was moving from making things with AI to being found by it. The fourth said access had replaced capability as the constraint. The fifth said AI made marketing faster than it made it better; the month's only long-run record says better, not faster. On the magazine's reading, the evidence backed the third on money and plumbing, and the first on search. By early October AI discovery looked like an addition to search, paid for and permissioned faster than its measurement kept up.

Our reading // of checked facts

On the magazine's reading, if a model is a brand's first reader, the job adds being cited, permitted and measured to producing assets, and September moved the first two. Being measured lagged.

A short shortlist

On 7 September Latent Space published its Frontier AEO Tracker, which ran 7 models across 161 categories. GPT-6 Astra searches a median of 5 sources per answer, against 9 for Sol, 11 for Opus and 15 for Fable. Those are sources searched; the tracker doesn't say how many end up cited. Latent Space's reading was that Anthropic, which makes Opus and Fable, is tuning its models to look wider. How long the shortlist is depends partly on whose model is reading. Only 28 of the 161 categories have one pick that every model agrees on. In the other 133, the first pick depends on which model is asked.

So the shortlist looks short, and it changes with the reader. For a brand, whether it is visible in AI became a countable question: on which models, and for which questions.

Most US Google searches already ended without a click before September. From January to April 2026, 68% of them ended without one, up from 60% in 2024, by SparkToro's count, though the two years come from different panels. Ranking didn't mean being cited, either. In a March study of 4 million AI Overview citations across 863,000 searches, Ahrefs found that only 37.9% of the cited URLs ranked in the top 10 for that search.

Nor had making things with AI stopped being work. In Knak's 2026 survey of 333 enterprise marketers, 88% said AI content needs moderate or substantial editing, and 85% had missed at least one campaign launch date in the past 12 months. Tom Tunguz's five-year record of his own writing, published on 2 September, showed AI lifting his weakest posts the most, as scored by an AI panel, without cutting his time to ship. Better, not faster. On the magazine's reading, being found added a job to the marketing team without taking the old one away.

The money arrived

On 10 September IAB raised its forecast for growth in all US ad spend this year to 12.3%, from 9.5% in January. Optimising content for AI-generated answers became buyers' top area of increased focus, at 76%. Focus on using generative AI in media campaigns slipped from 78% in January to 69%, still most buyers. Buyers' top media investment challenge, cited by 44%, was adapting to changing consumer behaviour, with AI-driven search as one part of it.

On measurement, buyers are changing, or about to. IAB found 86% of buyers changing, or expecting to change within 12 months, how they measure media because of AI tools and agents. The most common change, chosen by 48%, is measuring brand visibility and citations inside AI tools.

The new channel was already selling. OpenAI's David Dugan, its head of global ads solutions, said on 31 August that ChatGPT ads had passed a $1B run rate, less than seven months after the US pilot began on 9 February. On 16 September OpenAI began testing Sponsored Agents.

What the ads return is less clear. MediaPost found advertisers paying anywhere from under $3 to $13 a click, with no performance benchmarks across advertisers, industries or campaign types. In May, Criteo reported AI-referred conversion rates approaching twice those of traditional search, and click-through rates about three times those of comparable formats. Those are Criteo's own figures, and PPC Land noted that independent verification at scale wasn't available.

Walls, rules and plumbing

Access got rules while its traffic was still hard to count. From the weekend of 19 and 20 September, Amazon blocked Meta's Muse agent from shopping on amazon.com, saying unauthorised AI access breaks its conditions of use. On 22 September IAB Tech Lab published OpenProposal, a proposed specification for buyer and seller agents to exchange and evaluate media proposals, covering the stage from brief to commitment. Comments close on 22 October, and nothing is final until then.

The plumbing kept arriving. On 28 September Shopify opened its checkout to browser agents, and Adobe forecast that AI traffic to US retail sites will grow 130% this holiday season.

Each of those gates was set by a platform or a standards body. What a brand controls is narrower: its structured product data, its checkout and, for media buyers, a comment on OpenProposal.

Found, but not counted

The traffic that already exists is hard to see. Across the 73 online stores Shero Commerce manages, AI visits stayed flat for a year while orders from those visits rose 2.74 times. With the visits flat, each one was bringing in 2.74 times as many orders. A report of visits alone would have shown no change.

AI visits stayed flat for a year. Orders from those visits rose 2.74 times.

A Profound study, reported by MarTech on 1 October, found that after an AI assistant mentions a brand, that person visits the brand's site at 1.5 to 2.5 times their expected rate over the following seven days. Only about 2.5% of those visits carried a tag saying AI had sent them. The rest arrived unlabelled. An analytics report that counts tagged AI referrals would show about one in 40 of the visits that followed an AI mention. That is why The Operator's guide (12) has you date your mentions and watch direct and organic traffic for the week after.

On top of search

Our reading // of checked facts

On our reading, the month's evidence on the biggest question leans one way: AI is being added to search. MarTech reported on 1 October that Datos's State of Search report for the second quarter of 2026 found AI and traditional search growing at about the same rate, quarter over quarter. IAB still forecasts paid search growing 8.1% this year.

SOCi's local-search data shows both in use at once. The share of consumers who had used AI to find a local business in the past 30 days rose from 9% to 52% in a year. But 83% still use search to find local businesses, and 67% of consumers who use AI tools say they've been given wrong information about a local business. SOCi published its findings on 24 September, and MarTech carried them on 5 October.

Where the machine does read first, it talks back. In a Semrush survey of 2,338 consumers, reported by MarTech on 18 September, nearly 81% of chatbot shoppers had decided against a purchase on the AI's advice. Only 29% of consumers use chatbots for product research at all. On 2 October Forrester wrote that it is still unclear whether a critical mass of consumers want agents to buy for them. Its authors suggest specialised uses, such as Prime Day or holiday promotions, might be where adoption comes.

What to do this month

If you moved budget out of search to chase AI visibility, the data doesn't back you yet. What it does support is measuring the new channel properly, and The Operator's guide to being found, in 12, sets out the weekly log.

Plan for two readers: the model that reads first, and the search box that most people still use to find a local business.

The claim here most likely to be wrong is that AI discovery is growing on top of search rather than replacing it.
