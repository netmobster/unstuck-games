The Rules

Part one // I–VI

What we publish

I
Every fact has a source.

Every number, quote, date and attribution was checked against its source: the drafts on 5 October, and the lines the edit changed, on 6 October. Where we couldn't settle a line, its page says so.

The testcan you get from this sentence to the page it came from?
This issue 647 lines checked · 5 open, named on their pages
II
A summary is not a source.

We read the thing itself, not a description of it. Two numbers we printed in September came from summary boxes on two of Tom Tunguz's pages, which his site's own markup labels AI summaries, not from the essays underneath.

The testdid we read the primary page, or something that read it for us?
This issue No result line: Rule II has no line this issue
III
We test our own argument and publish the loss.

When we're wrong, the correction runs in print: what we said, what's true, how we found out. We don't quietly edit.

The testcould you reconstruct exactly what we got wrong from the page?
This issue 19 corrections to what we'd published · all in print
IV
A steelman says it's a steelman.

The case against our argument is written to win, and labelled as in-house. When an outsider argues with us, they get the same space and a reply.

The testwould someone who holds this view sign it?
This issue One steelman, written in-house and labelled · no outside case yet
V
A reading says it's a reading.

Where we go past what our sources say, to what the facts add up to or where they lead, the page says so: our reading, or our bet. Nothing we inferred is printed as if a source had said it.

The testcan you tell, from the page alone, where the evidence ends and our judgment begins?
This issue [N] passages labelled our reading or our bet · count and check pending
VI
Every piece tells you how to prove it wrong.

Each of the five main features ends with its weakest claim and the evidence that would change our mind.

The testafter reading it, do you know what would change our mind?
This issue 5 features, 5 Prove us wrong boxes · collected on one page, with a sixth for the grades
Part two // VII–IX · separation of duties

How it gets made

Each wall is a rule: work crosses it, the role shouldn't. Rule VII's line says where this issue's grades didn't keep to it
VII
The one who finds doesn't grade.

The agents that research a claim never check their own work. Separate agents do, from the sources.

The testfor every checked line and every claim's grade, was the grader someone other than the finder?
This issue 10 checker agents, none checking its own work · all on one model family · then a second (ChatGPT), 6 Oct The grades // first given by the agents that researched the claims · regraded by one that didn't
VIII
The one who grades doesn't rewrite.

Checkers mark each line confirmed, wrong or unconfirmed. They don't touch the prose. The rewrite comes after the check.

The testcan you see the verdicts separately from the edit that followed them?
This issue Each checked section's verdicts in the Receipts' table · most on their own stamp too
IX
The one who writes doesn't send.

No agent publishes, schedules or sends anything. A person says yes to each thing that goes out, and one yes covers one thing.

The testfor everything that went out, can you name who said yes, and when?
