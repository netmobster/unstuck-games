Squint

Two players. Three cards each. You never play a card. The whole game is betting about what you hold.

  1. The dealer puts up 1, the other puts up 2. The dealer speaks first — that is what the smaller stake buys.
  2. You may not stand still. Once you owe nothing, your only moves are to raise, to see, or to fold.
  3. Two raises each, per hand. When both are spent, only fold and see remain. That is what bounds a hand.
  4. See costs double what you owe. Both hands turn over. If you see and lose, they never show. Not that hand, not later.
  5. Blind — before you look, you may declare. You stake half, and you cannot be seen. Look whenever you like; you are then open, at full stakes.
  6. Knock the table any time. It costs nothing and does nothing, and the rules do not say what it means. He can knock too. Neither of you has been told what it is for.

    The coin only sizes a hand. This is what the series is for, and the game will not adjudicate what it means — that is between the two of you.
