# Squint

**A two-player wagering game where no card is ever played.** Each player holds three cards from a
40-card deck (1–10 in Coins, Cups, Rods, Blades). The whole game is public betting about what you
might be holding: fold, match, raise, or pay to *see*.

## The rules that make it Squint
- **Nobody plays a card.** The cards only exist to be lied about.
- **If you pay to see and lose, the winner never shows.** Permanently. You paid for an answer
  and got silence. The whole game rests on this.
- **Blind play:** bet before you look, at half stakes, and you can't be seen.
- **The knock:** a two-way tap on the table that means nothing by rule. Meaning shows up by
  pattern, and the ledger records it.
- **A series, not a hand:** race to 5, with a jackpot either player can propose and the other
  can refuse for free.
- **Provably fair:** the shuffle is committed before the first card and revealed at the end, so
  anyone can check the deal.

## The stake ladder
Coin · a possession · a favour · **an answer** · **a secret** · *something that was never yours
to wager*. You describe the stake in your own words, and **the game never rules on what it
means.**

## Why it's a roleplaying tool, not a tavern minigame

Gwent is a card game set *in* a world: you collect decks, win rounds, earn rewards, and the story
waits until you're done. **Squint is a scene.** Everything it produces feeds back into the story.

1. **It's an interrogation in disguise.** With no cards played, the only thing in play is the
   other person: their nerve, their tells, when they fold and when they push. Playing Squint
   *is* reading a character.
2. **Information is the currency.** A losing see buys you nothing, not even the truth. That's
   the same rule as Seren's fog: what's hidden stays hidden until the story earns it.
3. **The stakes are story hooks.** "One question, answered honestly." "The ledger page."
   "A favour, collected later." Win and you learn something. Lose and you owe someone.
   Either way the campaign changes.
4. **NPCs play by temperament, not by odds.** Seren's first opponent, Noke, "folds when it is
   about cards and escalates when he is made to wait, or looked past." You learn who he is by
   gambling with him, and the ledger proves it.
5. **The table can turn, but the result can't.** An NPC may refuse a stake, accuse you of
   cheating, or storm off. That can end a game, but it can never change who won a hand.
   Drama with honest bookkeeping.
6. **It lives in the world.** It uses the campaign's own deck, and the player always carries a
   Squint pack with that campaign's card backs. At Blood and Ink's wake you could deal the
   Bureaucrat in, stake "an answer", and play for what's in Agnes's ledger.

**In one line:** Gwent asks who has the better deck. Squint asks who you'd trust with a secret,
and makes you bet on it.

## Where it stands
- **Built:** a playable single-player version against Noke (an artifact, 20 Sep), after 558
  simulated hands with nobody playing. See `HANDOFF.md`.
- **Plan:** a standalone Unstuck game first (Jay, 20 Sep), then a Seren 1.5 feature: "Squint
  inside Seren" (SAI-589). The trust model comes first for two human players.
- **Not built:** table actions (refusing, accusing, leaving), stakes climbing between series,
  two-player.
